# PawPop restart: every direction worth considering, and one recommendation

Written 2026-10-20 from the 8 research reports in `research/web/` and `research/course/frameworks.md`, the team's interviews (`research/interviews.md`), and the professor's feedback (`research/earlier-work/README.md`). Seven verification agents were still re-checking the reports' unverified items when this was written; any number below that a verifier later corrects must be updated here (log: `research/verification/`).

## The answer in one paragraph

The project should stop selling "everything for dog owners" and prove one thing first: **getting a city dog the right company**. That means small, matched play sessions of 3 to 6 compatible dogs, which pop up in borrowed space (a church hall at 7 a.m., a closed restaurant's backyard, a dog run at an off-peak hour). Each dog earns a **Paw-ssport**, a record written in the dog's own voice that says who it plays well with and what it needs ("I love slow sniffers. Big bouncy boys scare me."). Dogs that want no company get a private "Quiet Pop" instead. The name PawPop already fits: it is a pop-up. The passport is the whimsical part, and it is also the practical one, because it travels with the dog to walkers, sitters, daycares, and landlords, who today handle every dog blind. The team's own strongest interview theme, the city's data, the science, and the competitor gaps all point here. Confidence: moderate-high.

## The recommendation in detail (direction A)

**Customer (one segment first).** Upper West Side and Morningside Heights owners whose dogs do not fit the free-for-all dog run: nervous or reactive dogs, small dogs, senior dogs, and new owners unsure how their dog plays. About 1 in 6 dogs fears other dogs (Finnish data; moderate as an NYC guide).

**Decision drivers** (what they choose on, per the interviews and online voices): the dog's safety and fit; trust built through people who have met the dog; being close to home; low cost; times that fit work (the off-leash hours before 9 a.m.).

**The one primary service.** A matched "Pop": 3 to 6 dogs, grouped by play style, size, and energy, for 45 minutes, run by a certified reward-based trainer, in borrowed space.

**The event that creates value before any network.** The "First Sniff": a dog's first Pop, where the trainer watches how it plays and stamps its Paw-ssport. One Pop with 6 dogs is a complete product on day one.

**The Paw-ssport.** A small booklet (and a web page) written in the dog's voice: who it plays well with, what scares it, food rules, and who can vouch for it. Stamps record each Pop. It answers three documented gaps at once: daycare tests that never leave the building, professionals who handle dogs blind, and the city's own call for a "pet resume". The team's earlier whiteboard said dogs are "often dehumanised"; the passport gives each dog a voice.

**For dogs who want nobody.** A "Quiet Pop": a private slot in the same borrowed space, plus a yellow "give me space" bandana that the Pops teach the neighborhood to read.

**How the person changes (the professor's question).**

| Dimension | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Emotional | Dread on every walk; "will my dog get rushed, or rush someone?" | Relief; the owner knows who the dog plays well with |
| Social | Random encounters at the run, or isolation for owners of nervous dogs | A small, steady pack of people who know the dog by name |
| Practical | Every walker, sitter, and daycare starts from zero | The passport speaks for the dog at every handoff |
| Identity | "Owner of the bad dog", or the anxious new owner | A member of a pack; the owner of a dog whose needs are known and normal |

Core meanings delivered (Shedroff's 15): Security, Community, Validation, Oneness, Duty.

**Chapter 2 (later, not the first service).** Owners who trust each other from the Pops form building or block care circles that swap walks and sitting (direction B), using the passport at every handoff.

**Business model (for the strategy slide).** Pay per Pop or a small monthly membership (Sniffspot's membership pivot is the precedent). Partners: the volunteer groups that run each of NYC's 95 dog runs, churches and schools with idle space, reward-based trainers, and neighborhood vet clinics that are losing visits and want new clients.

**The pilot.** One Pop at a Riverside Park dog run or a borrowed Morningside room, 6 to 8 dogs. Success means at least 3 owner pairs swap numbers, at least 2 playdates happen within a week without the team arranging them, and owners rate their own stress lower afterward.

## The strongest case against the recommendation

1. **Frequency.** A Pop is weekly at best, while the top daily pain online is care coverage (direction B). Answer: A is the trust engine B needs; B fails without it, as the skeptical replies to a 2026 swap pitch show.
2. **Liability.** A bite at a Pop is someone's problem; one 2025 insurance claim for bite wounds paid $59,700. Answer: certified trainers, small groups, fence-first introductions, and Quiet Pops for dogs that should not mix.
3. **Labels can hurt.** Owners may reject a passport that implies their dog is a problem. Answer: write it as "plays best with" in the dog's own voice, never "bad with".
4. **Owners of easy dogs do not need it.** The free dog run works for them. Answer: that is fine; the first segment is the dogs the run fails.
5. **Evidence gap.** Nobody has tested matching dogs into friend groups. Answer: that makes it a real innovation question, and the pilot is the test.

## Course frameworks this direction lets the team show

- **Five kinds of value and core meanings:** the earlier deck covered only functional and emotional; A adds identity ("my dog's needs are normal") and meaning (security, community).
- **Stakeholder value map:** the dog, trainers, dog-run volunteer groups, space hosts, walkers, vets, and neighbors who bear bites and off-leash dogs.
- **Moments of truth:** the first meeting between two strangers' dogs, which was the lowest point on the team's earlier waveline.
- **Waveline:** a current line that dips at "rushed by an off-leash dog" and "turned away by daycare", and an ideal line with a story arc that builds through First Sniff, first stamp, first friend, first handoff.
- **Fogg:** owners are motivated, but ability fails (where, when, and with whom is it safe?); the Pop is a facilitator.
- **Fixed SWOT:** strengths measured against Rover, the free dog run, Sniffspot, and daycares on the drivers above.
- **Service innovation (Jones and Samalionis):** prototype the extreme proposition early and pilot in one neighborhood.
