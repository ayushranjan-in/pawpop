# The pet industry, its economics, and the trends that matter (US first, global where useful)

Status: COMPLETE as of 2026-10-20. Gaps are listed in "What I could not verify" near the end.

Direct answer: the strongest evidenced dog-owner problem in the US is paying for vet care and not knowing the options or the fair price. Insurance, separation anxiety, food allergy, and travel paperwork are real but felt by a minority. New York already has well-funded modern vet clinics, and Wag!, an on-demand dog-walking app, went bankrupt in 2025, so a project should not build a clinic, a telehealth service, or a stranger marketplace. The evidence supports an in-person, matched dog meetup as a launch event, with help at the "can I afford this, and what are my options" moment. Details, sources, and confidence levels follow.

How to read this report:
- Every fact carries a source link, plus the publication date where the source shows one.
- Confidence levels: high (a primary source checked directly, or several reputable sources that agree), moderate (one reputable secondary source, or a primary source read only through search extracts), low (a single weak source, or my own arithmetic or inference).
- "Unverified" marks a claim I could not confirm. Such claims are not used in the conclusions.

## 1. Size and shape of the US pet industry

### Total spending
- US pet owners spent $158.0 billion in 2025, up from $151.9 billion in 2024. APPA states the growth as 3.7%, but its own two totals give 4.0%, and APPA does not explain the gap; this report quotes APPA's stated 3.7%. The American Pet Products Association (APPA, the industry's trade group) projects $165 billion for 2026, about 4.4% growth, of which about 2 percentage points are inflation. Data age: calendar 2025, released March 26, 2026. Sources: [APPA 2026 State of the Industry page](https://americanpetproducts.org/2026-state-of-the-industry) (2026); [APPA press release, March 26, 2026](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/u.s.-pet-industry-reaches-158-billion-in-2025-poised-for-continued-growth-in-2026) (opened 2026-10-20); [Pet Age, May 11, 2026](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/). Confidence: high.
- Growth is modest in real terms. If about 2 points of the projected 4.4% is inflation, real growth is about 2.4% a year. Confidence: moderate (my arithmetic on APPA's own split).

### Spending by category (APPA figures)

| Category | 2024 | 2025 | Share of 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food and treats | $65.8B | $68.3B | 43% |
| Vet care and product sales | $39.8B | $41.0B | 26% |
| Supplies, live animals, over-the-counter medicine | $33.3B | $34.4B | 22% |
| Other services (boarding, grooming, insurance, training, pet sitting, pet walking) | $13.0B | $14.3B | 9% |

- "Other services" is the smallest category but grew fastest (about 10% in 2025, from $13.0B to $14.3B). Food grew about 3.8%, supplies about 3.3%, and vet care about 3.0%.
- Sources: APPA's [Industry Trends and Stats page](https://americanpetproducts.org/industry-trends-and-stats) (opened 2026-10-20) for 2025, and the [Wayback Machine copy of the same page from October 4, 2025](http://web.archive.org/web/20251004205139/https://americanpetproducts.org/industry-trends-and-stats) for 2024. APPA's footnote says other services "includes boarding, grooming, insurance, training, pet sitting, pet walking." The 2025 categories add to exactly $158.0B and the 2024 categories to exactly $151.9B. [Vet Advantage](https://vet-advantage.com/vet-advantage/by-the-numbers-u-s-pet-industry-reaches-158-billion-growth-expected-to-continue-in-2026/) (May 2026) repeats the 2025 figures. Confidence: high.

### Who owns pets
- 95 million US households owned at least one pet in 2025. Dog households rose from 51% of households in 2024 to 53% in 2025 (71 million households, about 4 million more than a year earlier). Cat households reached 53 million (39% of households, up 5%). Sources: [APPA 2026 State of the Industry](https://americanpetproducts.org/2026-state-of-the-industry); [Pet Age, May 11, 2026](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/). Confidence: high.
- The American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) counts about 87.3 million dogs and 76.3 million cats in US homes in 2025, from its 2025 AVMA Pet Ownership and Demographics Sourcebook. Sources: [AVMA News, "Evolving pet owner economics," October 15, 2025](https://www.avma.org/news/evolving-pet-owner-economics-what-data-reveal-veterinary-teams) (opened 2026-10-20); also cited in [NAPHIA's 2026 report, page 5](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf) (June 21, 2026). Confidence: high.
- By generation (APPA 2025 report, describing 2024): Millennials are 30% of pet owners, Gen X 25%, Baby Boomers 25%, and Gen Z 20%. APPA's release says: "Gen Z comprised 20% (18.8 million) of U.S. households that owned a pet, a 43.5% increase from 2023." 70% of Gen Z pet owners have two or more pets. 58% of Gen Z dog owners and 63% of Millennial dog owners are men. Sources: [APPA 2025 State of the Industry release](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/the-american-pet-products-association-appa-releases-2025-state-of-the-industry-report-archive) (dated March 26, 2025, opened 2026-10-20); APPA's stats page in its October 4, 2025 archive copy for the generation table. The differing split seen elsewhere (Gen Z 21%, Millennials 30%, Gen X 28%, Boomers 22%) is APPA's newer 2025 data, now shown on its [live stats page](https://americanpetproducts.org/industry-trends-and-stats). Confidence: high.
- Gen X "empty nesters" drove much of 2025's ownership growth (Gen X dog ownership up 12% year over year, stated in APPA's March 26, 2026 release). "Gen Z now represents 22% of cat owners, up 10% year over year." Sources: [Pet Age, May 11, 2026](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/); [Pet Food Industry, "Report: Cat ownership grows to 53 million U.S. households," September 15, 2026](https://www.petfoodindustry.com/pet-food-market/market-trends-and-reports/news/15834866/report-cat-ownership-grows-to-53-million-us-households), citing APPA's 2026 Cat Report (opened 2026-10-20). Confidence: high.

### Budget pressure
- 22% of pet owners said they spent less on their pets in 2025, and about half said their spending did not change. Owners are trading down to cheaper food, grooming at home, and cutting treats. Gen Z and Millennials were more likely to say the economy did not affect their pet ownership. Sources: [Pet Age, May 11, 2026](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/); the 22% figure also appears in [APPA's March 26, 2026 release](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/u.s.-pet-industry-reaches-158-billion-in-2025-poised-for-continued-growth-in-2026). Confidence: high.

### Pets as family
- Pew Research Center surveyed "5,073 U.S. adults, including 2,963 pet owners, from April 10 to 16, 2023." 62% of Americans own a pet. 97% of owners say their pets are part of the family, and 51% say their pets are "as much a part of their family as a human member." Source: [Pew Research Center, July 7, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/07/07/about-half-us-of-pet-owners-say-their-pets-are-as-much-a-part-of-their-family-as-a-human-member/) (Pew's site refuses automated requests, so the page was read in full through its [Wayback Machine copy of January 4, 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/07/07/about-half-us-of-pet-owners-say-their-pets-are-as-much-a-part-of-their-family-as-a-human-member/); Pew last modified the page on April 24, 2025). Confidence: high. Data age: 3.5 years.
- The "as much as a human" view is strongest among urban owners (61%, versus 47% suburban and 50% rural), lower-income owners (64%, versus 46% middle-income and 43% higher-income), and women (57%, versus 43% of men). Same source. This matters for a New York City project: urban owners are the group most likely to treat a dog as a full family member. Confidence: high.

## 2. Veterinary care

### Prices keep rising faster than inflation
- In the 12 months to September 2026, US prices for veterinarian services rose 4.6%. All consumer prices rose 3.7%, human medical care services rose 2.5%, and pet food rose 1.5%. Source: [Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Consumer Price Index, Table 2](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm), released October 14, 2026. Confidence: high. Data age: 1 month.
- The longer run is steeper. In the raw BLS series for veterinarian services (CUUR0000SS62054), annual-average prices rose 8.8% in 2022, 9.4% in 2023, and 7.4% in 2024. On a December-to-December basis the same years give 8.8%, 10.8%, and 6.2%, and December 2024 to December 2025 gave 7.1%. From 2014 to 2024 (annual averages), vet services rose 62.3% while all consumer prices rose 32.5%. [PBS NewsHour (Paul Solman, July 1, 2026)](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/as-veterinary-costs-climb-private-equity-ownership-of-clinics-draws-scrutiny) reported that "veterinary care costs have risen by roughly 60 percent since 2014, far outpacing overall inflation," which matches the BLS series. Source: [BLS data for series CUUR0000SS62054 and CUUR0000SA0](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SS62054) (pulled 2026-10-20). Confidence: high.
- Higher prices are now cutting visits. AVMA News reports "revenue increases of about 2.5% in 2025 even as visits were down roughly 3%, continuing a four-year trend," and "81% of surveyed veterinarians reported clients were more sensitive to costs than they were in 2024 when the figure was 72%." The figures come from John Volk of Brakke Consulting, from a survey of about 350 respondents. Source: [AVMA News, "Veterinarians report increasing price sensitivity, decreasing visits," by R. Scott Nolen, February 13, 2026](https://www.avma.org/news/veterinarians-report-increasing-price-sensitivity-decreasing-visits) (opened in the Brave browser 2026-10-20). Confidence: high.

### Many owners skip care they were told their pet needs
- PetSmart Charities and Gallup surveyed 2,498 US dog and cat owners (November 2024 to January 2025). 52% skipped or declined veterinary care in the past year: 37% visited but declined a recommendation, and 15% did not go at all. 71% of those named cost as the key factor. 73% of them were not offered a lower-cost option. 66% of owners said they could afford $1,000 or less for lifesaving treatment. Only 23% had ever been offered a payment plan, and 64% said an interest-free plan would let them pay at least double. Source: [Gallup, April 15, 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/659057/pet-owners-skipped-declined-veterinary-care.aspx). Confidence: high.
- A University of Tennessee survey of 5,038 pet families (4,748 in English from March 14 to April 16, 2024, and 290 in Spanish from September 9 to 14, 2024) found that the share of families unable to get care rose since 2017: preventive care 26% (from 23%), sick care 18% (from 14%), emergency care 12% (from 8%). For preventive care (1,247 families who hit a barrier), cost was the top reason (57%), then no available appointments (23%) and no provider open at usable times (18%). "More than one in four respondents (26%) who reported at least one type of barrier to veterinary care indicated that they had experienced at least one of the negative outcomes." Among those 1,865 families, 11% surrendered a pet over behavior concerns plus lack of vet access, 9% surrendered a pet because they could not afford or reach vet care, 9% had a pet die, 7% euthanized a pet over vet access, and 4% euthanized a pet for lack of behavior resources. Source: [University of Tennessee Program for Pet Health Equity, "Access to Veterinary Care: Barriers and Insights from Pet Families"](https://trace.tennessee.edu/utk_socipubs/39/), August 29, 2025 (full PDF read). The 2018 baseline study, released December 17, 2018, found that "one out of four (27.9%) households experienced barriers to veterinary care" in the prior two years: [Access to Veterinary Care Coalition, 2018](https://www.maddiesfund.org/assets/research/access-to-veterinary-care-report-2018.pdf) (PDF read). Confidence: high.

### The "spectrum of care" movement
- "Spectrum of care" means a vet offers a range of acceptable options, from basic and cheaper to advanced and expensive, instead of only the "gold standard." The AVMA House of Delegates wrote this idea into its ethics code as "contextual care" on June 21, 2024: "A veterinarian should be prepared to offer a range of diagnostic, treatment, and when deemed appropriate, referral options that meet the needs of both the patient and the client," and when "less advanced" options are chosen, "an acceptable standard of care for those options" must be met. The May 2023 version of the code had no such section. Sources: [AVMA Principles of Veterinary Medical Ethics](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/avma-policies/principles-veterinary-medical-ethics-avma) (opened 2026-10-20); AVMA News report of the June 21 vote (R. Scott Nolen, June 23, 2024). The Journal of the AVMA published the supplement "Weighing the Evidence and the Options: Spectrum of Care" (volume 263, issue S3) on December 1, 2025, and the vet schools' association (AAVMC) "established its Spectrum of Care Initiative (SOCI) in 2021 with support from The Stanton Foundation" (AVMA News, August 8, 2023; [AAVMC initiative page](https://www.aavmc.org/resources/spectrum-of-care/)). Confidence: high.
- The gap between policy and practice is the Gallup finding above: 73% of owners who declined care for cost reasons were not offered a cheaper option.

### Corporate consolidation
- Mars Inc. owns Mars Veterinary Health, which runs Banfield Pet Hospital (mostly inside PetSmart stores), VCA Animal Hospitals, and BluePearl specialty and emergency hospitals. Economic Liberties counts "more than 2,500 clinics worldwide and 2,000 across the nation"; Mars Veterinary Health's own site now says 3,000 clinics worldwide, and Banfield alone has more than 1,000 hospitals. Mars bought VCA in an "all-cash transaction valued at approximately $9.1 billion," including $1.4 billion of debt (announced January 9, 2017), and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) required Mars "to divest 12 veterinary clinics around the United States that provide specialty and emergency services." Sources: [VCA SEC filing, January 9, 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000817366/000119312517005171/d321268dex991.htm); [FTC press release, August 30, 2017](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2017/08/ftc-requires-mars-divest-12-veterinary-clinics-condition-acquiring-pet-care-company-vca-inc). Confidence: high.
- JAB Holding owns NVA (National Veterinary Associates) and Ethos Veterinary Health. In June 2022 (orders of June 13 and June 29) the FTC required JAB to sell 11 clinics in six areas in connection with its Sage ($1.1B) and Ethos ($1.65B) deals, and for 10 years to get FTC approval before buying specialty or emergency clinics within 25 miles of its own in California, Colorado, D.C., Maryland, Texas, and Virginia. Holly Vedova, director of the FTC's Bureau of Competition, said private equity uses roll-ups to "accrue market power off the Commission's radar." Source: [AVMA News on the FTC actions, June 24, 2022](https://www.avma.org/news/firms-anticompetitive-acquisition-veterinary-clinics-prompts-ftc-actions). Confidence: high.
- Other private-equity-backed groups: Thrive Pet Healthcare (TSG Consumer Partners, since April 2020), PetVet Care Centers (KKR, since 2017), Mission Pet Health (the December 2024 merger of Southern Veterinary Partners and Mission Veterinary Partners, renamed in July 2025, owned by Shore Capital; [Shore Capital page](https://www.shorecp.com/companies/mission-pet-health)), and VetCor (majority owned by Oak Hill Capital since June 15, 2018; [Oak Hill release](https://oakhill.com/2018/06/15/oak-hill-capital-partners-leads-recapitalization-of-vetcor-alongside-harvest-partners-cressey-company-and-management/)). Confidence: high.
- Estimates of corporate share vary. The American Economic Liberties Project told regulators that corporate and private-equity owners held about 8% of practices "a little more than a decade ago," now 25% to 30% (Brakke Consulting estimate) or 49% (Sundale Research, 2023), with 50% of revenue and about 75% of specialty practices, which "include but are not restricted to emergency care" ([Economic Liberties comment to FTC and DOJ, September 20, 2024](https://www.economicliberties.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AELP-Comment-on-FTC-DOJ-RFI-Roll-Ups-9.20.24-FINAL.pdf), PDF read). [PBS NewsHour (July 1, 2026)](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/as-veterinary-costs-climb-private-equity-ownership-of-clinics-draws-scrutiny) gives a similar picture: "only 10 percent of vet practices a decade ago," now "from about 30 to as much as 50 percent," and 75% in specialty care. Confidence: moderate (industry and advocacy estimates; no official US count exists).

### The UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) investigation
The CMA is the UK's competition regulator. It ran a full market investigation into vet services for household pets and published its final decision in March 2026. Source: [CMA summary of final report (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/veterinary-services-for-household-pets-final-decision-report/summary-of-the-final-report-html), dated 25 March 2026 on the page (24 March in the PDF). Confidence: high.
- Findings:
  - Average vet prices rose 63% from 2016 to 2023, while general inflation was 32%.
  - Large vet groups owned about 10% of UK practices in 2013 and about 60% by 2025. The six groups are CVS, IVC Evidensia, Linnaeus, Medivet, Pets at Home, and VetPartners.
  - Prices at five large groups were together 18.3% higher than at independent practices.
  - Four groups made profits well above their cost of capital for a sustained period.
  - Only 47% of large-group customers knew their practice belonged to a large group. Over 40% of practices showed no prices online in 2025.
  - Common medicines cost 50% to 60% less at online pharmacies, worth £200 to £300 a year to an owner.
  - The CMA put consumer harm at around £1 billion over five years.
- Remedies: published price lists, written estimates for treatment over £500, itemised bills, disclosure of who owns each practice, a cap on written-prescription fees (£21 for the first medicine and £12.50 for each additional one), written prescriptions within 48 hours, and complaint and mediation processes.
- Rejected: general price controls, profit caps, and forced sale of practices.
- The binding order was made on September 22, 2026 and came into force the next day. Large businesses (15 or more practices or out-of-hours centres) must publish practice information, price lists, and pet care plan details by December 22, 2026; ownership information by March 22, 2027; and provide written estimates, itemised bills, and written prescriptions by June 22, 2027. Small businesses have until March or September 2027. Sources: [GOV.UK order page, first published September 22, 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/veterinary-services-market-investigation-order-2026-and-veterinary-services-market-investigation-royal-college-of-veterinary-surgeons-undertakings-202); [notice of making the order](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab13fb64c0f475de0a66aeb/notice_of_making_an_order__1_.pdf); [explanatory note](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab14876d52ca1fccea560af/explanatory_note__2_.pdf). Confidence: high.
- Relevance: the CMA documented the exact pain the team heard (opaque prices, second opinions that cost "hundreds and hundreds of dollars," no way to compare). No US regulator has done anything similar, so US vet prices remain hard to compare. That opacity is a real, evidenced problem.

### Telehealth
- Whether a vet may start a relationship with a new patient by video depends on state law. That relationship is called the veterinarian-client-patient relationship (VCPR), and without it a vet cannot diagnose or prescribe. As of October 2026, nine states explicitly allow it by video: Arizona, California, Florida, Idaho, New Jersey, Ohio, Vermont, and Virginia (the eight named by the ASPCA in March 2026), plus Rhode Island, whose law was signed on June 25, 2026 and allows prescriptions for up to 30 days. Washington State's law takes effect on July 1, 2027. Washington, D.C. has no rule either way: its practice act has "no VCPR language whatsoever," so nothing there ties a video start to an in-person exam. New York is not on the list. Its rule sits in the State Education Department's practice guidelines (guideline 5.11), which require the vet to know the patient through "a timely examination of the patient" or visits; the state board considered allowing a video start in 2023 and deferred it in December 2023, and the state vet society, which opposes a video start, is seeking a law that "will require an in-person visit" (board minutes, October 3, 2025). In practice, a New York vet must see a new patient in person before diagnosing or prescribing by video. Sources: [APPA blog on telemedicine, March 16, 2026](https://americanpetproducts.org/blog/the-future-of-veterinary-care-telemedicine-movement-in-2026); [ASPCA press release, March 25, 2026](https://www.aspca.org/about-us/press-releases/aspca-commends-washington-gov-ferguson-signing-bill-increase-access); [Providence Business News, July 26, 2026](https://pbn.com/mckee-holds-signing-ceremony-celebrating-approval-of-veterinarian-telehealth/); [California Veterinary Medical Board AB 1399 FAQ](https://www.vmb.ca.gov/licensees/ab1399_faqs.shtml) (in effect since January 1, 2024); [Vetster state list, updated October 17, 2026](https://vetster.com/en/wellness/what-us-states-allow-online-vet-prescriptions); [Veterinary Virtual Care Association map](https://vvca.org/the-vvca-telemedicine-map/); [NYSED veterinary practice guidelines](https://www.op.nysed.gov/sites/op/files/prof/vetmed/vetmedpg.pdf); [NY State Board for Veterinary Medicine minutes, October 3, 2025](https://www.op.nysed.gov/sites/op/files/2025-10/Vet%20Med%20Minutes%2010-03-2025.pdf). Confidence: high.
- The AVMA opposes video-only relationships. At its July 17 to 18, 2025 session, the AVMA House of Delegates adopted a revised model practice act "with 96% of the vote," which says "telemedicine must be conducted within the context of a VCPR that has been established in person." Source: [AVMA News, July 24, 2025](https://www.avma.org/news/revised-model-veterinary-practice-act-adopted). Confidence: high.
- Implication: general advice ("is this normal?", "what portion should I feed?") is legal anywhere, and membership clinics already bundle 24/7 telehealth. A project should not build a telehealth service.

## 3. Pet insurance

All numbers in this subsection come from the North American Pet Health Insurance Association (NAPHIA, the insurers' trade group) [State of the Industry Report 2026 Highlights](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf), dated June 21, 2026, covering calendar 2025. I read all 32 pages directly. Willis Towers Watson compiles the data, which NAPHIA says covers about 99% of US and Canadian pet insurance premium. Confidence: high.

### How many pets are insured
- 6.98 million US pets were insured at the end of 2025, up 9.0% from 6.41 million in 2024 and up about 76% in five years (page 17).
- That is 5.23 million dogs (up 6.8%) and 1.75 million cats (up 16.3%) (page 18).
- Only 4.27% of US pets are insured: 5.99% of dogs and 2.29% of cats (page 5). Roughly 94 of every 100 US dogs have no insurance.
- Growth in insured pets is slowing: 22.1% (2022), 17.2% (2023), 12.7% (2024), 9.0% (2025) (page 17).

### How much people pay
- US gross written premium (the total premium insurers booked) reached $5.68 billion in 2025, up 19.7% (page 12). Premium grows about twice as fast as the number of insured pets because the average premium keeps rising (page 17).

| Average annual premium (US) | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2025 change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dog, accident and illness | $625 | $640 | $675 | $749 | $836 | +11.5% |
| Cat, accident and illness | $370 | $387 | $383 | $386 | $435 | +12.6% |
| Dog, accident only | $192 | $201 | $204 | $193 | $190 | -1.6% |
| Dog, plan with built-in wellness | $1,051 | $1,134 | $1,263 | $1,321 | $1,414 | +7.0% |

(Page 22.) The average dog accident-and-illness premium rose 34% from 2021 to 2025, and more than 23% in the last two years alone. $836 a year is about $70 a month.

### New York's place in the market
- New York State holds 7.4% of all insured US pets and 8.1% of US premium, second only to California (18.1% and 19.9%) (page 25). New York owners pay above-average premiums, since their share of premium exceeds their share of pets.

### What gets claimed
- The most common dog claim conditions in 2025 were, in order: gastrointestinal problems, ear infections, skin conditions, anxiety and behavioral or neurological issues, allergies, growths and masses, eye disorders, diarrhea, urinary tract infections, musculoskeletal problems, exams and wellness, and vomiting (page 28). Anxiety and behavior rank fourth for both dogs and cats.
- Single claims can be huge. The ten largest dog claims paid in 2025 ranged from $42,300 to $66,600. They included a 4-year-old toy poodle mix in New Jersey with bite wounds ($59,700) and a 9-year-old Old English Sheepdog in Brooklyn with anemia ($54,700) (page 29).

### Interview check
- One interviewee's premium rose from $30 to $97 a month ($360 to $1,164 a year). $97 a month is about 39% above the 2025 national average for a dog accident-and-illness plan ($70 a month). More than tripling over a few years is far above the industry's average yearly rise (11.5% in 2025), so it most likely combines general premium inflation with the pet's age and a high-cost region. I did not find a source that gives typical age-based premium increases, so this explanation is unverified.

### Nationwide drops about 100,000 pets (2024)
- In 2024, Nationwide, then the largest US pet insurer, said it would not renew about 100,000 pet policies, slightly under 10% of its customers. Policies ended at their renewal dates from spring 2024 through summer 2025. Nationwide blamed rising veterinary costs "and other factors," said age, breed, and claims history played no part, and said it would focus on partnerships with Petco, Walmart, and Unum. One owner's letter said: "Increases in pet healthcare costs have made it infeasible to maintain certain insurance plans moving forward." Sources: [KGET / Nexstar, 2024](https://www.kget.com/news/state-news/nationwide-insurance-to-non-renew-100000-pet-insurance-policies/); [Fox Business, 2024](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/nationwide-dropping-pet-insurance-100000). Confidence: high.
- Owners filed a federal class action in Massachusetts on June 4, 2025. It cites Nationwide's old website promise, "We promise not to drop your pet because of age." One review site reports the court dismissed the case on November 20, 2025, while other sources still call it active. Sources: [Boston 25 News, 2025](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/25-investigates-mass-dog-owners-suing-nationwide-after-pet-insurance-policies-dropped/GT6EKQPPD5AZXDYVMVS3CSOENE/); [ClassAction.org, 2025](https://www.classaction.org/news/nationwide-lawsuit-alleges-insurer-wrongfully-canceled-whole-pet-policies-due-to-age-medical-conditions). Confidence: high (lawsuit filed), low (current status).
- The trap: a dropped owner of an older or sick pet cannot easily switch, because any new insurer treats the pet's existing conditions as pre-existing and excludes them.

### Premium hikes and complaints
- [NBC News (2023)](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/personal-finance/pet-insurance-dogs-cats-rising-prices-denied-claims-rcna93933) documented owners facing large renewals, including New Jersey owners told premiums could rise 33.6% and an owner whose premium for a Labrador rose 38% without a single claim. Confidence: moderate (search extract of the article).
- Trupanion, a large insurer, raises prices by groups of similar pets (breed, enrollment age, location) rather than by the pet's birthday. Search extracts report California approved Trupanion increases of roughly 12%, 29%, and 33% over about two years, and New York approved an 18% increase. Confidence: low (I could not open the regulator filings).

### What pet insurance does not cover
- The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC, the body of state insurance regulators) adopted a Pet Insurance Model Act in 2022. It mainly forces disclosure. It does not ban exclusions. Under the model, insurers may still exclude pre-existing conditions (anything a vet advised on, treated, or saw signs of before the policy started or during the waiting period), must disclose whether they exclude hereditary, congenital, or chronic conditions, may not impose waiting periods for accidents, and may impose at most 30 days for illness or orthopedic conditions. Sources: [NAIC Pet Insurance topic page](https://content.naic.org/cipr-topics/pet-insurance); [NAIC model law state chart](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/model-law-state-page-633_1.pdf). Confidence: moderate-high.
- Common exclusions listed in the NAIC's regulator guide: congenital and hereditary conditions (for example hip dysplasia, heart defects, cataracts, diabetes), preventive and wellness care, dental care, vaccinations, flea prevention, spaying or neutering, and behavioral training or therapy. Some insurers will not accept pets above a certain age. Source: [NAIC, "A Regulator's Guide to Pet Insurance"](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/publication-pin-op-pet-insurance.pdf). Confidence: moderate (read through search extract).
- About 17 states have adopted the model as of the NAIC's 2026 chart. New York has not; a bill is pending. Confidence: moderate.
- Plain meaning for the project: insurance pays back after the owner pays the vet, excludes the conditions an owner already worries about, rarely covers routine care or behavior training, and can be repriced or withdrawn. It does not solve "I can't afford this bill today."

## 4. Shelters and surrender

### National shelter numbers, 2025 versus 2024
Source: [Shelter Animals Count (SAC, the national shelter data nonprofit), 2025 Annual Data Report](https://www.shelteranimalscount.org/2025-report), released early 2026, covering calendar 2025. Confidence: high (fetched directly).
- 5.8 million dogs and cats entered US shelters and rescues in 2025 (2.8 million dogs and 3.0 million cats), down 2% from 2024 (121,000 fewer animals).
- Strays made up 59% of intake (60% in 2024). Owner surrenders made up 30% (29% in 2024). Other routes made up 11%.
- 4.2 million animals were adopted (2.0 million dogs, 2.2 million cats). The dog adoption rate rose from 55% to 57% of dog intake.
- 638,000 animals went back to their owners (down 3%). 19% of all dog intakes and 34% of stray dogs went home, versus only 6% of stray cats.
- 757,000 animals had a non-live outcome (down 1%). Shelters euthanized 320,000 dogs and 277,000 cats.
- The country has 13,471 sheltering organizations: 4,100 shelters and 9,371 rescues. Shelters handle 84% of intake.
- Mid-2025 snapshot: animals still in care had waited a median of 47 days (dogs) and 43 days (cats). Source: [SAC 2025 Mid-Year Report](https://www.shelteranimalscount.org/explore-the-data/2025-mid-year-report) (2025). Confidence: moderate (read through a search extract).

- The ASPCA (which Shelter Animals Count joined in 2025) says the shelter euthanasia rate fell from 10% in 2019 to 8% in 2025, and names lack of "access to veterinary care, pet-friendly housing or behavioral support" as what pushes owners to surrender. Source: [ASPCA, US Animal Shelter Statistics](https://www.aspca.org/helping-shelters-people-pets/us-animal-shelter-statistics) (2026, 2025 data). Confidence: high.

What this means: the shelter crisis is easing slightly but is not over. One in three shelter animals is a surrender by an owner, and that share is rising.

### Why owners give up dogs
- I did not find a recent national breakdown of surrender reasons (for example, what share is housing versus cost versus behavior). The ASPCA names the same three drivers (vet access, housing, behavior support) without percentages. Treat any precise national split you see online as unverified unless it names its study.
- The cost driver is measurable from the vet-access data in section 2: among families who hit a barrier to vet care, 26% reported outcomes such as giving up the pet or euthanasia ([University of Tennessee, 2025](https://trace.tennessee.edu/utk_socipubs/39/)), and 11% of owners in the Gallup study know someone forced to give up a pet over vet costs ([Gallup, 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/659057/pet-owners-skipped-declined-veterinary-care.aspx)).

### The pet-friendly housing shortage
Source: [Pet-Inclusive Housing Initiative reports page](https://www.petsandhousing.org/pet-inclusive-housing-reports/) (fetched 2026-10-20; it summarizes reports from 2021 to 2026, and the page does not tie each figure to a specific report year). Confidence: moderate.
- 72% of renters report difficulty finding pet-friendly housing, and 80% of dog owners reported difficulty securing housing.
- 79% of rental properties claim to allow pets, but fewer than 10% are truly "pet-inclusive" (no breed or weight limits), and only 8% of units are free of all pet-related restrictions.
- 69% of pet-owning renters name fees as a key barrier, and monthly "pet rent" is the most burdensome cost.
- 22% of renters surveyed had to rehome a pet, and 12% hid a pet to get housing.
- Relevance: New York City is widely described as a city of renters (I did not verify the renter share in this report). For renters, breed and weight limits, pet rent, and moving are a real risk to keeping a dog. This problem is big, but a student project cannot change landlord policy.

## 5. Time and care: work schedules, walkers, daycare, Rover and Wag!

### New York workers are back in the office more than in recent years
- Kastle Systems tracks office occupancy in 10 large US cities from keycard and app entries of over 300,000 users, measured against a pre-pandemic baseline from early 2020 (so 66% means 66% of the early-2020 level, not 66% of desks). For the week ending September 30, 2026, average occupancy was 55.4% across the 10 cities and 66.0% in New York City. For September 2026 as a whole, every city averaged above its own 2024 to 2026 average; the 10-city figure was 2.9 points higher and New York City led at 9.2 points higher. The national peak day (Tuesday, September 29) reached 65.5%. Source: [Kastle Back to Work Barometer](https://www.kastle.com/safety-wellness/getting-america-back-to-work/) (rolling page, opened in the Brave browser 2026-10-20; it still showed the week ending September 30). Confidence: high.
- What it means: more New York dogs spend more weekday hours alone than during 2021 to 2023, so weekday care demand is rising.
- The closest rigorous study of routine change is UK-based. Harvey and colleagues followed 1,807 UK dogs from the first 2020 lockdown (May to July 2020) to the easing of restrictions (October to November 2020). Separation-related behavior fell overall from 22.1% to 17.2%, but 9.9% of dogs developed new signs, and dogs whose time alone had dropped most during lockdown were at higher risk. Source: [Harvey et al., "Impact of Changes in Time Left Alone on Separation-Related Behaviour in UK Pet Dogs," Animals, February 15, 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani12040482) (abstract read through Europe PMC). This supports a link between owners' routine changes and new separation problems. No study measures the 2023 to 2026 US return to office itself (a Europe PMC search returned 161 papers, none on that question), so claims of a "return-to-office separation anxiety epidemic" in New York remain unproven. Confidence: high for the UK study; low for any New York effect size.

### Separation anxiety is real but affects a minority of dogs
- A study of 13,700 dogs in Finland (owner questionnaires) found that 72.5% of dogs showed at least one highly problematic behavior. Noise sensitivity was the most common (32%). Fearfulness affected 29%, including fear of other dogs (17%) and fear of strangers (15%). Aggression affected 14%. Separation-related behavior was the least common trait, at 6%. Source: [Salonen et al., Scientific Reports, March 5, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7058607/). Confidence: high for the Finnish sample, moderate as a guide to New York dogs.
- In US insurance claims, "anxiety, behavioral condition, or neurological issues" ranked fourth among dog claim conditions in 2025 ([NAPHIA 2026, page 28](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf)). Confidence: high.
- Relevance to the interviews: the worry "which dogs does my dog get along with" has a measurable basis. About 1 in 6 dogs fears other dogs. That is a minority, but a large one.

### The size of the walking, sitting, and daycare market
- APPA's "other services" category (boarding, grooming, insurance, training, pet sitting, and pet walking) was $14.3 billion in 2025, the fastest-growing category at about 10% (see section 1). Confidence: high.
- APPA counts insurance inside "other services," but the size of its insurance slice is not published. Subtracting NAPHIA's $5.68 billion of US insurance premium would leave about $8.6 billion for everything else, yet IBISWorld alone puts grooming and boarding at $11.3 billion and dog walking at about $1.4 to $1.5 billion in 2026 (next bullet). The subtraction therefore does not hold, and APPA most likely counts much less than the full insurance premium. Confidence: high that the subtraction fails; unknown how APPA sizes insurance.
- IBISWorld's public industry pages (opened 2026-10-20) give these 2026 figures. [Pet Grooming and Boarding in the US](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/pet-grooming-boarding/1735/) (July 2026; this industry includes boarding and daycare): $11.3 billion, growth of 3.6% a year from 2021 to 2026, and 1.6% expected in 2026. [Dog Walking Services in the US](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/dog-walking-services/4350/) (June 2026; includes pet sitters who visit homes): "roughly $1.4 billion in 2026" in the analyst summary and "$1.5bn in 2026" in the same page's answers section, growth of 3.0% a year from 2021 to 2026 and 0.7% in 2026, 59,279 businesses, and no company holding more than 5% of the market. IBISWorld does not publish a separate dog-daycare figure on its public pages. Confidence: moderate (one market-research firm; the full reports are paywalled).

### Rover: bought by private equity
- Rover was incorporated in Seattle on June 16, 2011, and acquired its rival DogVacay in March 2017. It went public through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company (a listed shell company), Nebula Caravel Acquisition Corp., which closed on July 30, 2021; its shares began trading on Nasdaq as ROVR on August 2, 2021. Rover's revenue was about 21% to 22% of the value of bookings on its platform ($95.1 million on $436.4 million in 2019, and $109.8 million on $521.9 million in 2021). Source: [Rover Group 10-K for 2021, filed March 21, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1826018/000182601822000034/rovr-20211231.htm). Confidence: high.
- Blackstone's funds bought Rover "in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately $2.3 billion" ($11.00 a share). The deal was announced on November 29, 2023 and closed on February 27, 2024, and Rover left Nasdaq. Source: [Blackstone press release, February 27, 2024](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/blackstone-completes-acquisition-of-rover/) (opened in the Brave browser). Confidence: high.
- Rover bought the London cat-sitting service Cat in a Flat on October 31, 2024 ([PR Newswire, October 31, 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rover-group-acquires-cat-centric-sitting-marketplace-cat-in-a-flat-302292107.html)). Brent Turner, Rover's president, replaced co-founder Aaron Easterly as CEO effective July 1, 2025, as announced on June 12, 2025 ([GeekWire, June 12, 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/rover-ceo-aaron-easterly-stepping-down-after-leading-pet-sitting-giant-since-2011/), opened in the Brave browser). Confidence: high.

### Wag!: bankrupt
- Wag Labs launched on-demand dog walking in Los Angeles in 2015. SoftBank's Vision Fund invested $300 million in early 2018 (CNBC dates the first investment to January 2018) for a stake of nearly 50%, and in December 2019 sold that stake back to Wag. The price was not disclosed; the Wall Street Journal reported it was below the $650 million valuation at which SoftBank invested. Sources: [Los Angeles Times (Bloomberg), December 9, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2019-12-09/softbank-sells-wag-stake-dog-walking); [CNBC, December 9, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/12/09/softbank-sells-stake-back-to-wag.html) (both opened in the Brave browser). Confidence: high.
- In February 2022, Wag announced a "$350 million business combination" with CHW Acquisition Corporation, a special-purpose acquisition company. The deal closed on August 9, 2022, and the shares began trading on Nasdaq as PET on August 10, 2022. Sources: [Wag press release filed with the SEC, February 3, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1842356/000110465922011175/tm225162d1_ex99-1.htm); [Wag Form 8-K, filed August 15, 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1842356/000110465922091417/tm2221514d1_8k.htm). Confidence: high.
- Wag filed a pre-packaged Chapter 11 case in the US Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on July 21, 2025 (case 25-11358). Its filings do not cite a shift in consumer demand. They say Wag had "incurred net losses" every year since its founding ($38.6 million in 2022, $13.3 million in 2023, and $17.6 million in 2024), that a sale process from September 2024 to May 2025 found no buyer, and that it could not refinance its debt. Its main secured lender, Retriever LLC, took ownership: the court confirmed the plan on August 29, 2025, and on September 1, 2025 the old shares were cancelled. The shares had moved from Nasdaq to over-the-counter trading on July 31, 2025. Confidence: high.

### What happened and why (my interpretation)
- Wag bet on on-demand walks by strangers, funded by venture capital, and went bankrupt. Rover built repeat relationships and in-home boarding, and private equity bought it. Both stories say the same thing: pet-care marketplaces are hard to make profitable, and the valuable asset is a trusted, repeat caregiver, not on-demand matching.
- This matches the team's interview finding that owners trust people they know over star ratings. Confidence: low-moderate (interpretation, not a sourced finding).

## 6. Travel with pets

### The CDC dog import rule (in force since August 1, 2024)
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) sets the rules for dogs entering or returning to the US.
- Every dog must be at least 6 months old at entry, carry a microchip that a universal scanner can read, appear healthy on arrival, and have a receipt from the online CDC Dog Import Form. The form can be filled in on the day of travel or up to six months ahead, and the receipt stays valid for six months and multiple entries if the dog has not been in a high-risk country. For dogs coming only from rabies-free or low-risk countries, that form is the only required CDC document. Source: [CDC, "Dogs arriving from dog rabies-free or low-risk countries"](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/rabies-free-low-risk-countries.html), updated July 22, 2024. Confidence: high.
- A dog that was in a high-risk country for dog rabies in the past six months and is not vaccinated against rabies will not be allowed into the US. Source: [CDC dog importation page](https://www.cdc.gov/importation/dogs/index.html), updated August 1, 2024. Confidence: high.
- A US-vaccinated dog that will return from a high-risk country needs a "Certification of U.S.-Issued Rabies Vaccination" completed before it leaves the US. It "cannot be issued retroactively." Source: [USDA APHIS pet export page](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/us-to-another-country-export), dated August 14, 2026. Confidence: high. This is a trap: an owner who forgets the form before the trip cannot fix it abroad.
- Foreign-vaccinated dogs from high-risk countries face extra steps (blood tests, specific airports, a CDC-registered animal care facility). I did not open those pages, so the details are unverified here.

### Taking a dog abroad (USDA APHIS health certificates)
The USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) endorses the health certificates other countries require.
- APHIS tells owners to "contact a USDA-accredited veterinarian as soon as you decide to travel." The accredited vet works out the destination's requirements, prepares the health certificate, and submits it to APHIS for endorsement through APHIS's online system (VEHCS). Sources: [APHIS pet travel page](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel) (March 20, 2026); [APHIS pet export page](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/us-to-another-country-export) (August 14, 2026). Confidence: high.
- APHIS endorsement fees, certificate time windows for specific countries, and typical accredited-vet charges were not on the pages I opened. They are unverified here. Only accredited vets can issue these certificates, which limits where an owner can go.

### Airlines, BARK Air, and relocation services
- Airline pet fees and size limits: not verified. The American Airlines page refused automated access and the JetBlue page returned no content. Check each airline's own page before quoting a number.
- BARK Air, a private-jet service for dogs and their owners run by the dog-products company BARK, launched in 2024. Quartz's 2024 headline called it "a new $6,000-a-flight airline for dogs." Source: [Wikipedia, "BarkBox"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BarkBox), citing Quartz (2024). Confidence: low-moderate. Its 2025 to 2026 prices and status are unverified (barkair.com returned no readable content).
- Professional pet relocation costs: not verified in this pass.

### How common is this problem?
- International travel with a dog is real, high-stakes, and confusing (one interviewee raised it), but it touches a small share of owners in any year. I found no verified count of US dogs that travel abroad. Treat it as a real problem felt by a minority.

## 7. Loneliness and health

### The public-health framing
- In May 2023, US Surgeon General Vivek Murthy issued an advisory, "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation," that likened the dangers of loneliness to smoking and obesity. Source: [Wikipedia, "Loneliness"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loneliness) and [Wikipedia, "Vivek Murthy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivek_Murthy), both citing the US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). Confidence: moderate.
- The advisory's own figures, read in the full PDF: "Recent surveys have found that approximately half of U.S. adults report experiencing loneliness, with some of the highest rates among young adults." "Lacking social connection can increase the risk for premature death as much as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day." Loneliness and social isolation raise the risk of premature death by 26% and 29% respectively, and poor social connection is associated with a 29% higher risk of heart disease and a 32% higher risk of stroke. The mortality impact is "even greater than that associated with obesity and physical inactivity." Source: [US Surgeon General, "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation," 2023](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf) (HHS refuses automated downloads, so the identical PDF was read through its [Wayback Machine copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)). The advisory does not discuss pets. Confidence: high.

### Do pets reduce loneliness? The evidence is mixed
- A systematic review of 24 studies found that "only a part of the studies detected a significant association between pet ownership, loneliness and social isolation," and that the COVID-19 pandemic seemed to strengthen the link. Source: [Kretzler, König and Hajek, Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology, October 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272860/). Confidence: high.
- Owners themselves feel the bond strongly: 97% call their pets family and 51% say "as much as a human member" ([Pew, July 7, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/07/07/about-half-us-of-pet-owners-say-their-pets-are-as-much-a-part-of-their-family-as-a-human-member/)).
- Owner polls by the American Psychiatric Association and the Human Animal Bond Research Institute (HABRI) that report mental-health benefits were not verified in this pass.
- Plain meaning: "a dog cures loneliness" is not a safe claim for a pitch. A safer claim is that owners feel their dog is family, and that people-to-people connection around dogs is a hypothesis the team can test, not a proven effect.

## 9. Cats

- Cat ownership is growing. 53 million US households (39%) owned a cat in 2025, up 5%, driven by Gen Z and Millennials. Gen Z makes up 22% of cat owners. Sources: [APPA 2026 State of the Industry](https://americanpetproducts.org/2026-state-of-the-industry); [Pet Food Industry on APPA's cat report](https://www.petfoodindustry.com/pet-food-market/market-trends-and-reports/news/15834866/report-cat-ownership-grows-to-53-million-us-households) (2026). Confidence: high for households, moderate for the Gen Z share.
- The AVMA's 2025 survey counts about 76.3 million cats and 87.3 million dogs in US homes ([NAPHIA 2026, page 5](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf)).
- Cats are far less protected than dogs. Only 2.29% of US cats are insured, versus 5.99% of dogs. But insured cats grew 16.3% in 2025 versus 6.8% for dogs, and cats are now 25.1% of insured pets ([NAPHIA 2026, pages 5 and 18](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf)). Confidence: high.
- Lost cats rarely get home. Shelters returned 34% of stray dogs to their owners but only 6% of stray cats in 2025 ([Shelter Animals Count 2025](https://www.shelteranimalscount.org/2025-report)). Confidence: high.
- The vet-visit gap: the classic study is the Bayer Veterinary Care Usage Study ([Volk, Felsted, Thomas and Siren, Journal of the AVMA, May 2011, DOI 10.2460/javma.238.10.1275](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.238.10.1275)); I confirmed the record through Europe PMC. I could not open its abstract or a current AVMA figure (the AVMA statistics page returned no readable content), so the size of the cat-versus-dog visit gap is unverified here.
- Relevance: cats are a growing, under-served adjacent group, but they do not walk outside, so ideas built around dog walks and dog meetups do not transfer to cat owners. The professor's suggestion to talk to cat owners is still useful as a contrast case.

## Top 10 findings that should shape the project

1. **Paying for vet care is the biggest, best-evidenced problem.** 52% of owners skipped or declined vet care in a year, 71% of them because of cost, and 73% of those were never offered a cheaper option ([Gallup, 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/659057/pet-owners-skipped-declined-veterinary-care.aspx)). Vet prices still rise faster than inflation (+4.6% versus +3.7%, [BLS, October 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t02.htm)). Confidence: high.
2. **Price opacity is a proven harm, not a hunch.** The UK regulator found 63% price growth from 2016 to 2023, 18.3% higher prices at large groups, and owners who did not know who owned their vet ([CMA, March 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/veterinary-services-for-household-pets-final-decision-report/summary-of-the-final-report-html)). The US has no equivalent rule, which leaves the "what should this cost, and what are my options" moment open. Confidence: high (UK), moderate (transfer to the US).
3. **Insurance does not solve this for most owners.** About 94% of US dogs are uninsured; the average dog premium rose 11.5% in 2025 and 34% since 2021; Nationwide dropped about 100,000 pets ([NAPHIA, 2026](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf); [KGET, 2024](https://www.kget.com/news/state-news/nationwide-insurance-to-non-renew-100000-pet-insurance-policies/)). The interviewee's jump from $30 to $97 a month is extreme but points the same way. Confidence: high.
4. **New York already has the "friendly modern vet," and it is consolidating.** Small Door ($149 a year membership) merged with Bond Vet into a 55-plus-clinic network in July 2026, and Chewy bought Modern Animal for $399.8 million. Do not build a clinic, a membership vet, or a telehealth service; New York is also not among the states that allow a video-only start to vet care. Confidence: high.
5. **Clinics are losing visits.** Visits fell about 3% in 2025 while revenue rose about 2.5%, the fourth year of that pattern ([AVMA News](https://www.avma.org/news/veterinarians-report-increasing-price-sensitivity-decreasing-visits)). A clinic network that needs new clients is a realistic partner or sponsor for a launch event. Confidence: high (data), low-moderate (partner inference).
6. **"Which dogs get along" is a measurable problem.** 17% of dogs fear other dogs and 14% show aggression ([Salonen et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7058607/)); behavior is the 4th most common dog insurance claim; one bite-wound claim cost $59,700. An in-person event that matches dogs carefully addresses a real need, and it must manage bite risk. Confidence: high (data), moderate (transfer to New York).
7. **Marketplaces for walkers and sitters are a graveyard or a private-equity asset.** Wag! filed for bankruptcy in July 2025; Rover sold to Blackstone for $2.3 billion. A marketplace is the classic "empty at launch" trap the professor warned about. Trusted, repeat, local relationships are the asset. Confidence: moderate.
8. **Urban owners are the most "dog as family" group in the country.** 61% of urban pet owners say their pet is as much family as a human member, versus 47% in the suburbs ([Pew, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/07/07/about-half-us-of-pet-owners-say-their-pets-are-as-much-a-part-of-their-family-as-a-human-member/)). Identity and meaning value are strong in New York. Confidence: high.
9. **Budgets are tightening.** 22% of owners spent less on their pets in 2025, and owners are trading down on food and grooming ([Pet Age, May 2026](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/)). Premium subscriptions face headwinds; cheap or free community value fits the moment. Confidence: moderate-high.
10. **Some interview themes are overstated or minority problems.** True food allergy affects only 1% to 2% of dogs ([Olivry and Mueller, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-017-0973-z)), so food worry is an information problem. Separation-related behavior affects about 6% of dogs in Finnish data (17% to 22% in a UK cohort with a broader definition). International travel paperwork touches few owners. Evidence that pets reduce loneliness is mixed ([Kretzler et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272860/)). Do not center the project on any of these. Confidence: high (data), moderate (judgment).

## Opportunities and threats this suggests

Each item names the evidence it rests on. "My inference" marks reasoning that goes beyond what a source states.

### Opportunities
1. **The "before you say no" moment at the vet.** Owners decline care without seeing cheaper acceptable options or payment plans. Evidence: 73% of cost-driven decliners were never offered a cheaper option, only 23% were ever offered a payment plan, and 64% said an interest-free plan would let them pay at least double ([Gallup, 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/659057/pet-owners-skipped-declined-veterinary-care.aspx)); the AVMA ethics code now backs "contextual care" ([AVMA](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/avma-policies/principles-veterinary-medical-ethics-avma)). Something that helps an owner ask for options, compare a quote, or get a second opinion addresses the strongest evidenced need.
2. **A matched, small-group dog meetup as the launch event.** It creates value on day one without a large network, which answers the professor's "empty at launch" question. Evidence: 17% of dogs fear other dogs and 14% show aggression ([Salonen et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7058607/)); behavior is a top-four claim category ([NAPHIA, 2026](https://naphia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NAPHIA_SOI2026_Report_HIGHLIGHTS_26-06-24.pdf)); urban owners see dogs as full family ([Pew, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/07/07/about-half-us-of-pet-owners-say-their-pets-are-as-much-a-part-of-their-family-as-a-human-member/)). My inference: the event also builds the trusted local relationships that later make other services work.
3. **A clinic as host or sponsor.** Evidence: vet visits fell about 3% in 2025 for the fourth straight year ([AVMA News](https://www.avma.org/news/veterinarians-report-increasing-price-sensitivity-decreasing-visits)), and New York's Small Door and Bond Vet network has 55-plus clinics ([PR Newswire, 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bond-vet-and-small-door-veterinary-merge-to-expand-access-to-trusted-veterinary-care-302821175.html)). My inference: a clinic that needs new clients may supply a vet or vet technician for a meetup in exchange for introductions.
4. **Trusted neighbor care circles instead of a stranger marketplace.** Evidence: Wag! went bankrupt and passed to its lender ([Wag SEC filings, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1842356/000184235625000098/pet-20250829.htm)), Rover's revenue was about 21% of the value of its bookings ([Rover 10-K for 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1826018/000182601822000034/rovr-20211231.htm)), New York office occupancy reached 66.0%, above the 55.4% 10-city average ([Kastle](https://www.kastle.com/safety-wellness/getting-america-back-to-work/)), and interviewees trust people they know. My inference: neighbors who met at an event can swap weekday walks and emergency cover.
5. **Plain answers to "is this normal?" from a trusted human.** Evidence: digestive problems, ear infections, skin problems, and allergies are the most common dog claim categories (NAPHIA), while true food allergy is rare ([Olivry and Mueller, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-017-0973-z)). General advice is legal without an in-person exam; diagnosis and prescriptions are not (see section 2).
6. **Small, high-stakes checklists (a feature, not the core).** Evidence: a US-vaccinated dog's rabies certification for a high-risk country "cannot be issued retroactively" ([APHIS, 2026](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/pet-travel/us-to-another-country-export)); renters face strict pet rules ([PIHI](https://www.petsandhousing.org/pet-inclusive-housing-reports/)). Useful for a minority, so better as add-ons than as the primary service.

### Threats
1. **Well-funded incumbents in New York vet care.** Small Door plus Bond Vet, Chewy plus Modern Animal, and Mars (Banfield, VCA, BluePearl). Anything clinic-like competes with them; anything vet-adjacent should partner with them.
2. **Regulation.** New York is not among the states that allow a video-only start to vet care ([APPA telemedicine blog, 2026](https://americanpetproducts.org/blog/the-future-of-veterinary-care-telemedicine-movement-in-2026)), and the AVMA opposes it. Remote diagnosis of a new patient should be treated as off the table.
3. **Marketplace economics and the empty-at-launch trap.** Wag! is the cautionary case (bankrupt July 2025).
4. **Tight budgets.** 22% of owners spent less on pets in 2025 ([Pet Age](https://www.petage.com/appa-report-pet-industry-consumer-spending/)). A paid subscription will be a hard sell.
5. **App fatigue and pet-tech consolidation.** Whistle's site now redirects to Tractive; Modern Animal sold to Chewy. A standalone app is the weakest form for a new idea.
6. **Bite and liability risk at meetups.** One 2025 bite-wound claim cost $59,700 (NAPHIA), and 14% of dogs show aggression (Salonen et al.). Event design must screen and match dogs.
7. **Overclaiming health benefits.** Evidence that pets reduce loneliness is mixed ([Kretzler et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9272860/)). A pitch that promises to cure loneliness will not survive scrutiny.
8. **Unstable insurance partners.** Insurers reprice and exit (Nationwide's 2024 non-renewals). An idea that depends on an insurer partnership rests on shifting ground.
