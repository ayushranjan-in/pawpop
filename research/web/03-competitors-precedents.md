# Competitors and precedents: what already exists, what died, and why

Research date: 2026-10-20. Status: COMPLETE. About 50 web searches and fetches; the session's search budget ran out before I could verify Care.com and NYC-only walker startups (marked unverified below).

## In short
- The team's three scrapped ideas all collide with something that exists or already died. The social and matching app is the most-failed idea in this space (Dogster's community closed in 2019, Tindog faded, Dig closed in 2022). The trusted-walker directory competes with Rover, which Blackstone bought for $2.3 billion in 2024 and which keeps buying rivals. The all-in-one building already exists as PetSmart with a Banfield vet inside (1,680+ stores) plus Small Door and Bond Vet clinics, which merged in July 2026.
- The interviewee's "drop-off spots across the city on one membership" was built in Brooklyn (DogSpot, about 40 to 50 sidewalk dog houses). The city's transportation department forced them off the sidewalk in 2018, and the 2019 law for a pilot appears never to have been carried out.
- 2025 to 2026 casualties: Wag! went through Chapter 11 bankruptcy (July to September 2025). Bark Social closed all its dog bars in November 2024 and reopened two under a new owner. Fetch Park closed a site. DOG PPL's Brooklyn club keeps missing its opening date. BARK's revenue fell 18.5%.
- What works: memberships that sell trust (BorrowMyDoggy, TrustedHousesitters, Sniffspot, which doubled its revenue after it switched to a membership), and short shelter-dog outings, which make a dog 5 times more likely to be adopted. NYC's shelter system already runs these outings (BoroughBreak).
- Best white spaces, with evidence: screened small-group play for nervous or reactive dogs in Manhattan; a portable "this dog plays well" credential; dog time for dog-less students supplied by shelter dogs; and event-first formats that create value before any network exists.

How to read this report:
- Every fact carries a source URL and, where the page shows one, its publication date. Pages without a date are marked "undated" or "accessed 2026-10-20". "Unverified" marks anything I could not confirm.
- Several facts come from search-engine summaries of pages I could not open directly (Inc. and Hepper blocked access). Those are flagged where they appear.
- Confidence levels: high (company page, filing, or reputable journalism agrees), moderate (one decent source), low (single weak source or inference).
- "Lesson" lines are my own analysis, not sourced facts.

## 1. Care marketplaces (sitters, walkers, borrowers)

### Rover (alive, private, owned by Blackstone)
- What it is: the largest US online marketplace that matches pet owners with independent sitters, walkers, and boarders. Most revenue comes from overnight stays (GeekWire, 2024).
- Business model: Rover takes a cut from both sides. Rover's own help center (accessed 2026-10-20) says sitters pay a 20% service fee and owners pay an 11% booking fee. On a $100 booking the owner pays $111, the sitter keeps $80, and Rover keeps $31 (about 28% of what the owner pays). California works differently: the sitter keeps the full rate they set, and the owner pays a 25% "marketplace fee" on top of that rate plus the 11% booking fee. A 2024 version of the help page capped the owner fee at $50 and listed a 25% sitter fee for RoverGO; the current page shows neither. Rover says some US sitters are in pilot programs with different fees ([Rover help center, "What are the service fees?"](https://support.rover.com/hc/en-us/articles/205385304-What-are-the-service-fees); [Rover help center, "Fees and rates in California"](https://support.rover.com/hc/en-us/articles/12439190554132-Fees-and-rates-in-California); [Wayback Machine copy of the fee page, about 2024-06](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://support.rover.com/hc/en-us/articles/205385304-What-are-the-service-fees)). Confidence: high.
- Consolidation: Rover keeps buying rivals. Since the Blackstone buyout it has made four purchases, all confirmed by Rover's own press releases:
  - Cat in a Flat, a London cat-sitting marketplace with 50,000+ care providers, announced 2024-10-31 ([PR Newswire, 2024-10-31](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rover-group-acquires-cat-centric-sitting-marketplace-cat-in-a-flat-302292128.html)).
  - Gudog, a dog-sitting and walking network of 20,000 sitters and walkers in eight European countries, announced 2025-04-29 together with Rover's launch in Denmark and Ireland ([Rover press release, 2025-04-29](https://www.rover.com/blog/press-release/gudog/)).
  - Mad Paws, the leading Australian marketplace and the main Australian competitor of Pawshake (300,000 active pet parents, 70,000 registered carers, 400,000+ transactions in 2024). Rover announced the deal on 2025-07-21 in Seattle (2025-07-22 on the Australian stock exchange) and completed it on 2025-11-11 at A$0.14 cash per share ([Rover announcement, 2025-07-21](https://www.rover.com/blog/press-release/mad-paws/); [Rover completion release, 2025-11-11](https://www.rover.com/blog/press-release/mad-paws-close/)).
  - Meowtel, a US cat-sitting marketplace with 4,000+ sitters, announced 2026-01-28; terms were not disclosed and Meowtel keeps its brand ([Rover press release, 2026-01-28](https://www.rover.com/blog/press-release/meowtel/); [GeekWire, 2026-01-28](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/rover-acquires-meowtel-a-cat-sitting-marketplace-in-the-u-s/)).
  - Confidence: high (company press releases, opened 2026-10-20).
- Traction: gross sales passed a $1 billion annual run rate in Q3 2023, with Q3 2023 revenue of $66.2 million (up 30% year over year) and net income of $10.5 million (GeekWire, 2024-02, citing company results).
- Status: Blackstone funds completed an all-cash buyout valued at about $2.3 billion on 2024-02-27, paying $11.00 per share; Rover had gone public through a SPAC (a "blank-check" shell company merger) in August 2021. Rover bought London cat-sitting marketplace Cat in a Flat on 2024-10-31 (see the purchase list above).
  - Sources: [GlobeNewswire, "Blackstone Completes Acquisition of Rover", 2024-02-27](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/02/27/2836062/0/en/Blackstone-Completes-Acquisition-of-Rover.html); [GeekWire, 2024](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/rover-ceo-on-companys-next-chapter-as-pet-sitting-giant-completes-2-3b-deal-with-blackstone/). Confidence: high.
- Lesson: the "find a trusted walker or sitter" job is owned by a $2.3 billion incumbent with a decade of reviews. A student "trusted directory" competes head-on with Rover and loses on supply.

### Wag! (alive but went bankrupt in 2025; now private, owned by its lender)
- What it is: on-demand dog walking and sitting app (San Francisco), plus pet insurance comparison and a vet-prescription tool called Furscription.
- Traction and money: once reportedly valued at $650 million; went public through a SPAC deal with CHW Acquisition Corp in 2022 at about $350 million; lost $69.5 million from 2022 through 2024 (CFO statement in court filings).
- Status: filed a pre-packaged Chapter 11 bankruptcy (a restructuring where the main creditor agrees to the plan in advance) on 2025-07-21 in Delaware. All old stock was cancelled. Its secured lender, Retriever LLC, took 100% ownership and the company emerged private on 2025-09-02, 66 days after filing. About $16.3 million of secured debt was converted. Operations (walking, sitting, insurance, Furscription) continued.
  - Sources: [GlobeNewswire, "Wag! Group Co. Successfully Completes Pre-Packaged Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization", 2025-09-02](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/02/3142423/0/en/Wag-Group-Co-Successfully-Completes-Pre-Packaged-Chapter-11-Plan-of-Reorganization.html); [SFGate, "San Francisco tech company, once worth $650 million, files for bankruptcy", 2025-07](https://www.sfgate.com/tech/article/sf-tech-company-wag-files-for-bankruptcy-20790355.php). Confidence: high.
- Lesson: on-demand walking is a low-margin gig marketplace. Even the number-two player, once valued at $650 million, could not make it pay. Do not pick "walkers on demand" as the primary service.

### BorrowMyDoggy (UK, alive)
- What it is: matches dog owners with nearby "borrowers" (dog lovers who walk, mind, or host the dog for free). Founded in London in 2012 by Rikke Rosenlund (CEO) and Les Cochrane after she looked after a neighbour's dog; 85 people signed up in the first three days ([BorrowMyDoggy About page](https://www.borrowmydoggy.com/about); [About Time Magazine, about 2014-03](https://www.abouttimemagazine.co.uk/life/borrowing-a-doggy/)).
- Business model and price: annual membership, no per-walk fee. Basic profiles are free; Premium costs GBP 48.99 per year for owners and GBP 12.99 per year for borrowers. Premium members are verified and get third-party liability insurance and a 24/7 vet phone line, but the insurance is "only valid when both parties (Owner and Borrower) are Premium members". Borrowers are never paid.
- Traction: a six-figure seed round (no more than GBP 350,000) and Seedcamp selection in 2013. In October 2015 it secured over GBP 1.5 million through Crowdfinders, a live investor pitch event (reported as an investment from IW Capital), and then closed its Crowdcube crowdfunding campaign.
- Member counts, exactly as the company states them. None is audited, and the company's website gives no number:
  - The company's LinkedIn About page (undated, accessed 2026-10-20) says "over 500,000 members throughout the UK and Ireland".
  - Founder and CEO Rikke Rosenlund's LinkedIn post of 2026-05-18 says the company has "built a community of over 1 million members across the UK and Ireland", and her LinkedIn headline says "1M+ members".
  - The company told the Daily Record (2025-07-23) that it has "over a million members", including "more than 100,000 borrowers" who signed up in Scotland.
  - In 2015 the company claimed over 250,000 members; in 2014 it told About Time Magazine it does not release member figures.
  - The website itself says only that it has "been trusted by thousands of owners for over 10 years".
  - So the company's current claim is "over 1 million members", and the older "over 500,000" LinkedIn text is stale. Neither number says how many members are active.
  - Sources: [BorrowMyDoggy membership page, accessed 2026-10-20](https://www.borrowmydoggy.com/membership); [UKTN, 2013-10-30](https://www.uktech.news/news/borrowmydoggy-20131030); [Crowdfund Insider, 2015-10-22](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2015/10/76127-borrowmydoggy-shuts-down-crowdcube-campaign-fetches-over-1-5m-through-crowdfunding-event-crowdfinders/); [Startups.co.uk, 2015](https://startups.co.uk/news/borrowmydoggy-lands-1-5m-crowdcube-funding/); [BorrowMyDoggy LinkedIn About page](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/borrowmydoggy); [Rikke Rosenlund LinkedIn post, 2026-05-18](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rikkerosenlund_yesterday-i-met-one-of-our-members-at-goodwoof-activity-7462081751234973696-cAzm); [Daily Record, 2025-07-23](https://www.dailyrecord.co.uk/news/scots-dog-lovers-swiping-right-35605746); [Dogonomist, 2024-12-19](https://www.dogonomist.com/p/4m-borrowing-dogs). Confidence: high on model and price; high that these are the company's own claims; low on the true number of active members.
- Lesson: this is the closest existing proof that "dog-less dog lovers" will show up for free, and that owners will pay a small annual fee for vetted strangers plus insurance. Note what makes it work: verification, insurance, and a membership fee that filters out unserious users. I found no verified US equivalent at comparable scale.

### TrustedHousesitters (UK-founded, global, alive)
- What it is: free pet sitting in exchange for free accommodation. Owners get a live-in sitter for their pets; sitters (often travelers) get a free place to stay. No money changes hands between them.
- Business model and price: annual membership on both sides. In US dollars (2026): sitters pay $129, $169, or $259 a year (Basic, Standard, Premium); pet owners pay $149, $229, or $299; a combined plan costs $209 to $399. UK membership starts at GBP 99. Since 2025-12-08, Basic and Standard members, both owners and sitters, also pay a booking fee of GBP 9 (US$12) each time a sit is confirmed, starting at their next renewal; Premium members do not pay it (company blog, 2025-12-08).
- Traction: 70,000 members in 2019, 100,000 in 2022, 200,000 by end of 2023; the About page claims 240,000+ members in 180 countries and 16 million+ nights of sitting; the company's own press release of 2026-05-27 says "over 280,000 members across 180 countries". Valued at about $100 million when Mayfair Equity Partners invested (late 2023). Member counts include inactive members, per the company's own forum.
  - Sources: [PR Newswire, "TrustedHousesitters finds new $100m home at Mayfair Equity Partners", 2023-12](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/trustedhousesitters-finds-new-100m-home-at-mayfair-equity-partners-302019456.html); [TrustedHousesitters About page, accessed 2026-10-20](https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/pages/about/); [TrustedHousesitters press release, 2026-05-27](https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/pages/love-at-first-sit-launch/); [TrustedHousesitters blog on the booking fee, 2025-12-08](https://www.trustedhousesitters.com/blog/news/booking-fee/); [House Sitting Magazine pricing review, 2026](https://housesittingmagazine.com/trusted-house-sitter-pricing-plans/). Confidence: high on model and fee; high that the member counts are the company's own figures, moderate on how many members are active.
- Lesson: membership plus a non-cash exchange (pets for a home) beats per-transaction fees when the parties are not professionals. Same pattern as BorrowMyDoggy.

### Pawshake (Australia and Europe, alive, small)
- What it is: a Rover-style sitter marketplace, strongest in Australia and parts of Europe.
- Model: commission per booking. Pawshake's own help centre says: "Out of each booking, we keep a contribution of 19%... You receive 81% of your advertised price." Owners also pay a separate service fee that varies with the booking and is capped for large bookings ([Pawshake help centre, sitter costs, undated, accessed 2026-10-20](https://support.pawshake.com/hc/en-nz/articles/115002030263-What-is-the-cost-of-registering-as-a-sitter); [Pawshake help centre, service fee](https://support.pawshake.com/hc/en-gb/articles/33949519870612-What-is-the-Pawshake-Service-Fee)).
- Traction: only about $1.5 million in disclosed funding (a seed round from AirTree and Slingshot on 2014-12-15, per Tracxn, updated 2026-09-12; PitchBook shows a different, higher total of $12.5 million from 7 investors) ([PitchBook Pawshake profile](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/101941-66)). The two databases disagree and Pawshake publishes no total. I found no acquisition or new funding in 2024 to 2026. Its main Australian rival, Mad Paws, was bought by Rover in 2025.
  - Sources: [Tracxn Pawshake funding, 2025](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/pawshake/___HVFmexUabFRQ6JDN1a2d2bv_dLt5xXuvQbhrWoFWew/funding-and-investors); [The Pet Sitter, "Australian Pet Sitting Market Consolidation 2026"](https://thepetsitter.co/en/blog/australian-pet-sitting-consolidation-2026); [Pawshake Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pawshake.android&hl=en_US). Confidence: moderate.
- Lesson: outside the US, the sitter category is consolidating around Rover. Sub-scale marketplaces survive but do not grow.

### Care.com pet care (alive; sold by IAC to a private equity firm in March 2026)
- What it is: Care.com lists pet sitters and dog walkers next to child care and senior care. Its NYC pet-care listings (accessed 2026-10-20) show sitters at about $8 to $50 an hour, most at $17.
- Price: browsing is free, but a family must buy Premium to message caregivers. Premium costs $39.99 a month billed monthly, $25.99 a month billed quarterly, or $13.99 a month billed annually; a featured job post costs $4.00 a month. Care.com's help article (2026-09-23) says plans vary by area.
- Ownership: IAC bought Care.com in February 2020 for $15.00 per share in cash, about $500 million. IAC then sold it to Care Parent, LLC, a subsidiary of the private equity firm Pacific Avenue Capital Partners, for a gross price of about $320 million. The sale was announced on 2026-03-02 and completed on 2026-03-16.
  - Sources: [IAC release on completing the purchase, filed with the SEC, 2020-02-11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/891103/000110465920015988/tm1926761d17_ex99-1.htm); [IAC Form 8-K announcing the sale, 2026-03-02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1800227/000110465926022305/tm267714d1_8k.htm); [PR Newswire, "Pacific Avenue Capital Partners to Acquire IAC's Care.com", 2026-03-02](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/pacific-avenue-capital-partners-to-acquire-iacs-carecom-302701509.html); [IAC Form 8-K on completing the sale, 2026-03-16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1800227/000110465926028545/tm268870d1_8k.htm); [Care.com plans and pricing, accessed 2026-10-20](https://www.care.com/app/vhp/plans-and-pricing); [Care.com NYC pet care listings, accessed 2026-10-20](https://www.care.com/pet-care/new-york-ny). Confidence: high.
- Lesson: pet care is a side category for a general care marketplace, not a focus. The marketplace was worth about $180 million less in 2026 than in 2020.

Adjacent failures worth knowing:
- Baroo, a dog walking and pet-sitting startup that sold through high-end rental buildings, appears in CB Insights' startup post-mortem list; it lost to better-funded rivals that served more cities ([CB Insights post-mortems](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/startup-failure-post-mortem/)). Confidence: moderate.
- App store reviews of a current playdate app (PupDate) say "once you get enough users, this app will take off", which is the empty-at-launch problem in users' own words ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pupdate-dog-play-date/id6741749936)). Confidence: moderate.

Lessons:
- Dogster reached scale in the 2000s and still lost its community features; no standalone dog social or matching app I found has reached durable scale since. The ones that survived (Rover, BorrowMyDoggy) attach the social contact to a concrete job (care, walking) and charge for trust.
- The earlier team idea, "a social app to meet nearby dog owners and match compatible dogs", is the single most-tried and most-failed idea in this whole space. Avoid it as the primary service.
- Density beats breadth: 18,000 users spread over 400 cities is an empty app everywhere. Any digital layer has to start in one neighborhood, attached to a physical event where people already are.

## 3. Place discovery and the reliability of "dog-friendly" labels

### BringFido (alive, profitable size unknown)
- What it is: a directory and booking site for pet-friendly hotels, rentals, restaurants, and attractions. Founded by Melissa Halliburton in Greenville, South Carolina (2005 per the company; she has also said the site launched the year after her team spent 2005 phoning every US hotel about pet policies).
- Business model: free to pet owners; BringFido earns commissions from hotels and travel partners, offers a "Pet Friendly Guarantee" (it will make it right if a hotel refuses the pet or overcharges the pet fee), pays affiliates a flat $5 per hotel stay, and sells pet amenity kits to hotels. Some Better Business Bureau complaints allege it kept a markup between what the customer paid and what the hotel received; unverified.
- Traction: claims "millions" of pet owners and 500,000+ pet-friendly places, including 150,000+ bookable hotels and rentals in 100+ countries with hand-checked pet policies. Revenue estimates disagree ($5 to 25 million per Owler; $32.5 million per RocketReach); treat as unverified.
  - Sources: [BringFido About page, accessed 2026-10-20](https://www.bringfido.com/about/); [SmarterTravel founder Q&A, undated](https://www.smartertravel.com/traveling-dogs-qa-founder-bringfido/); [BringFido affiliate agreement](https://www.bringfido.com/affiliate/agreement/); [BBB complaints page](https://www.bbb.org/us/sc/greer/profile/dog-boarding/bringfidocom-0673-90005189/complaints). Confidence: high on model, low on size.
- Lesson: the one place-discovery business that lasted 20 years did it by phoning places to verify policies and by earning travel commissions. Verification is the product.

### Google Maps "dogs allowed"
- In 2023, Google added a "Pets" attribute to Business Profiles showing whether dogs are allowed inside, outside, or both ([Search Engine Roundtable, 2023](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-pets-attribute-35834.html)). Confidence: high.
- Reliability: the attribute is a yes or no field that business owners and any user can suggest or change; Google updates Maps from third-party data and user feedback. No study measures how often it is wrong. A Google Local Guide reported Bangalore hotels marked pet-friendly that, when called, said the pet would be kept off-site ([Local Guides Connect, undated](https://www.localguidesconnect.com/t/how-one-local-guide-uses-maps-to-discover-pet-friendly-places/407063)). Marketers note that businesses gain clicks by adding attributes, so the incentive runs toward saying yes. Confidence: moderate (mechanism verified, error rate unknown).

### The NYC legal reality behind "dog-friendly" in NYC
- Restaurants: a 2015 New York State law (Public Health Law 1352-e, effective with the governor's signature on 2015-10-26) let restaurants choose to allow companion dogs in outdoor dining areas only. NYC adopted matching Health Code rules (Chapter 32) in 2016: dogs must stay within arm's length, on a leash or in a carrier; restaurants must post a sign and report bites to 311 within 24 hours; dogs may not touch tables. Indoors, live animals are banned except service animals (Health Code 81.25). In June 2025 the health department proposed extending the rules to roadway cafes and requiring 36 inches of dog-free aisle between tables; I could not confirm whether it was finalized.
  - Sources: [NY State Senate press release, 2015](https://www.nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2015/senate-passes-legislation-allowing-customers-bring-dogs-outdoor-dining); [Christian Science Monitor, 2015-10-27](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Society/2015/1027/Dogs-now-allowed-in-NY-restaurants.-What-are-the-rules); [NYC DOHMH notice of adoption, Chapter 32, 2016](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/notice/2016/noa-chapter32-dining-with-dogs.pdf); [NYC DOHMH notice of intention, 2025-06-18](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/notice/2025/noi-ch32-dogs-dining-areas-06-18-25.pdf). Confidence: high.
- Transit: MTA Rules of Conduct, 21 NYCRR 1050.9(h)(1), bar any animal on subways and buses "unless enclosed in a container and carried in a manner which would not annoy other passengers"; service animals are exempt. This is why New Yorkers carry large dogs in duffel bags ([NY Codes via Justia](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-21/chapter-xxi/subchapter-d/part-1050/section-1050-9/)). Confidence: high.

Lessons:
- In NYC, "dog-friendly restaurant" can only legally mean "dogs on the outdoor seating, if the owner allows it". A label that does not say patio-only, size limits, or busy hours is close to useless, and nobody publishes the details that matter to an owner of a large or nervous dog.
- Directories are crowded (BringFido, Google, Yelp, BarkHappy's map). The gap is not another list; it is trusted, specific, current detail.

## 4. Private and screened play

### Sniffspot (alive, profitable, growing)
- What it is: an "Airbnb for dog parks". Homeowners rent out fenced yards, fields, and indoor rooms by the hour to one dog owner at a time. It is popular with owners of reactive dogs (dogs that bark, lunge, or panic around other dogs or people) because nobody else is there.
- Business model and price: hosts set their own prices, on average $10 per dog per hour. Sniffspot takes 22% plus a payment processing fee (a $10 booking leaves the host about $7.34). At the end of 2024, after growth slowed, it moved new users to a membership: Sniffspot Membership starts at $20 per month, and new guests need it to book spots with 5 or more reviews.
- Traction: 32,000+ listed spots (company listings page, accessed 2026-10-20); about 7,000 hosts and 10,000 bookings a month in 2022; 20,000+ hosts across 3,600 cities in 2023; about 30,000 active hosts in 2025, versus roughly 6,000 public dog parks in the whole US (founder's figure). Revenue reportedly doubled from $3 million (2024) to $6 million (2025) after the membership switch. Total funding is only about $4.4 to $4.46 million (Andreessen Horowitz among investors), with no new capital since 2020; about 40 staff; cash-flow positive.
  - Sources: [Inc., July 2026 (could not open, figures from search summary)](https://www.inc.com/georgia-fearn/airbnb-dogs-sniffspot-revenue-pivot-memberships-pet-tech/91369447); [Sniffspot help center on fees, accessed 2026-10-20](https://help.sniffspot.com/article/32-how-much-does-sniffspot-charge); [Philadelphia Inquirer, 2025-05-29](https://www.inquirer.com/business/small-business/sniffspot-app-yard-rental-dogs-20250529.html); [CNBC, 2024-07-26](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/07/26/backyard-side-hustle-renting-out-land-as-private-dog-park-sniffspot.html); [GeekWire, 2023](https://www.geekwire.com/2023/testing-sniffspot-airbnb-for-dog-parks-gives-dogs-a-safe-place-to-play-and-homeowners-extra-cash/); [PitchBook profile](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/230711-41). Confidence: high on model and spot count, moderate on revenue (company-reported to Inc.).
- NYC gap: Sniffspot's supply is yards, and dense Manhattan has almost none. Its largest region is the West Coast (about a third of business, per the Inquirer). NYC supply is thin and mostly in outer boroughs and suburbs (not verified by count).
- Lesson: "a safe, empty space for a dog that cannot handle the dog run" is a real, paid, growing need. Sniffspot proves people pay $10 to $20 an hour for it. The lesson for NYC is that the scarce asset is private space near home, and the membership pivot shows that repeat users are where the money is.

## 7. Dog parking

### DogSpot, formerly Dog Parker (Brooklyn; status in 2025-2026 unverified, likely dormant)
- What it is: climate-controlled, app-unlocked "smart dog houses" on sidewalks outside stores, so owners can go into shops that do not allow dogs. Each had a phone-viewable camera and an ultraviolet light to sanitize between uses. Reported prices were 20 to 30 cents per minute in different years ("$1 per five minutes" in one report, "30 cents per minute" in another); units fit dogs up to 100 pounds.
- Why it existed: NYC Health Code rules bar pets from food service and food stores, so owners tie dogs to poles outside (BKReader, 2019-08-06).
- Traction: founded 2015 by Chelsea Brownridge and Todd Schechter; $1.1 million seed funding; about 40 units in NYC (other reports say "over 50" at peak and 1,000+ customer accounts).
- The NYC fight: in November 2017 the NYC Department of Transportation (DOT), not the health department, ordered the houses removed within two weeks because they "create obstructions on sidewalks that are clearly public property". The company pulled all 40 units in February 2018. Council Members Stephen Levin and Rafael Espinal attacked the decision. Some residents worried that dogs would panic locked inside, and the time limit had been cut from 3 hours to 90 minutes.
- The law that followed: Int. 0886-2018 became Local Law 150 of 2019 (enacted 2019-08-23), requiring a pilot of "pet harbors" next to businesses in one Brooklyn zone, within 3 feet of the building and removed overnight. DOT opposed it. The sponsor, Espinal, left the Council on 2020-01-26, and I found no evidence the pilot ever launched.
- After NYC: rebranded DogSpot and moved to private property (store-owned land): 10 New York State Thruway service areas (August 2018), a Stop & Shop pilot in Port Washington, NY (2019), plus QFC, Kroger, and Albertsons stores; 60+ units in 14 states by May 2019. I found no news after 2019. PitchBook and Craft list it as active but these are database entries, and Crunchbase shows the founder at Citi since September 2020.
  - Sources: [Patch, "High-Tech Dog Houses Must Go, DOT Tells Brooklyn Business", 2017-11](https://patch.com/new-york/bed-stuy/high-tech-dog-houses-must-go-dot-tells-brooklyn-business); [Brooklyn Eagle, 2018](https://brooklyneagle.com/65930/dog-gone-why-did-the-city-kick-out-the-digital-dog-houses/); [BKReader, 2019-08-06](https://www.bkreader.com/business-innovation/high-tech-dog-houses-make-a-comeback-on-brooklyn-sidewalks-6543930); [6sqft, 2019](https://www.6sqft.com/nyc-council-approves-pilot-program-for-air-conditioned-pet-harbors-outside-of-brooklyn-shops/); [NYC Council, Int 0886-2018](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=342A8A3A-9206-47EE-8E11-F524406C3635&ID=3498473&Options=&Search=); [GlobeNewswire, Stop & Shop pilot, 2019-05-28](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2019/05/28/1853346/0/en/stop-shop-to-pilot-high-tech-dog-house.html); [ABC7 NY, 2018](https://abc7ny.com/dogspot-dogparker-new-york-thruway-rest-area-for-dogs/3861535/). Confidence: high on history, low on current status.
- Lesson: the student interviewee's "drop-off spots across the city on one membership" was already built in Brooklyn, and it died on sidewalk law, not on demand. If the project revisits "dog parking", put it inside a partner's property with a human attendant, which turns it into micro-daycare.

## 8. Dog sharing and shelter outings

### The evidence that short outings work
- A peer-reviewed study (Gunter and colleagues, 2023, in the journal Animals) analyzed 51 US shelters: 1,955 dogs that went on brief outings or short foster stays, against 25,946 dogs at the same shelters. A brief outing made a dog 5.0 times more likely to be adopted; a short overnight foster made it 14.3 times more likely. Dogs were adopted an average of 10 days after their outing. Only about 4% of outing volunteers adopted the dog themselves (about 12% after overnight stays), so the effect comes from exposure, not from the volunteer. Programs that recruited more members of the general public did better.
  - Sources: [Gunter et al., "The Influence of Brief Outing and Temporary Fostering Programs on Shelter Dog Welfare", Animals, 2023-11](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/ani13223528); [Virginia Tech news release, 2023-11](https://news.vt.edu/articles/2023/11/cals-research-dog-shelter-adoption.html). Confidence: high.
- VCA Charities, which funds and coaches these programs, claims a single day out makes a dog "10 times more likely" to be adopted. That is double the peer-reviewed figure; use the study's 5x ([VCA Charities Dog Day Out starter guide, 2024-06](https://www.vcacharities.org/sites/g/files/fnmzdf4591/files/2024-07/VCA%20Charities%20Workbook_Dog%20Day%20Out%20Starter%20Guide_060324.pdf)). Confidence: high that the claims differ.

## 9. Signals for reactive dogs

### The Yellow Dog Project (alive as a volunteer campaign, no formal organization behind it at scale)
- What it is: an education campaign asking owners to tie a yellow ribbon, lead, bandana, or vest on a dog that needs space, and asking everyone else to keep their distance. Its founder coined "DINOS", short for "dogs in need of space". A dog may need space because it is fearful, reactive, in training, recovering from surgery, old, in pain, or a service dog. Yellow does not mean the dog is aggressive.
- Origin: Tara Palardy of Alberta, Canada started it in 2012 (PetMD says 2013), inspired by a Swedish "Yellowdog" program begun by behaviorist Eva Oliversson. Some trace the idea to Australian trainer Terry Ryan in 2009.
- Reach: in 2014 its Facebook page had 20,000+ followers and the project claimed reach in 45 countries (SPCA International). Wellington City Council in New Zealand explains the yellow ribbon on an official page.
- How widely it is recognized: no systematic survey exists. A German guide states plainly that recognition "has not been systematically surveyed" and that dog owners know it far better than the general public. Reactive-dog owners report it is "ignored most of the time", and bystanders mistake yellow leads for high-visibility gear. Yellow collars can also mean "up for adoption" in some shelters, so the signal collides with other color codes. It has no legal force anywhere I found.
  - Sources: [PetMD, undated](https://www.petmd.com/what-does-yellow-ribbon-dog-mean); [SPCA International, about 2014](https://www.spcai.org/news/feature-stories/yellow-ribbon-warning); [Psychology Today, 2014-12](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/all-dogs-go-heaven/201412/the-yellow-dog-project); [Wellington City Council](https://wellington.govt.nz/dogs-and-other-animals/dogs/yellow-ribbon-on-a-collar); [Unterhunds guide, 2026](https://www.unterhunds.de/en/guides/yellow-dog-project). Confidence: high on what it is, moderate on recognition (no data exists, which is itself the finding).
- Similar signals: yellow "give me space" vests promoted through a "#dogsinyellow" awareness day by reactive-dog owners ([Rescue Dog Rehab newsletter](https://rescuedogrehab.substack.com/p/happy-dogsinyellow-day-the-vest-that)), and leads printed with words like "Nervous" sold by small UK shops ([My Anxious Dog](https://myanxiousdog.co.uk/blogs/news/what-is-the-yellow-dog-project)). Same recognition problem. Confidence: moderate.
- Lesson: the reactive-dog problem is real enough that owners invented a global signal on their own, and the signal fails because the public never learned it. A signal only works if the people around the dog know it. That is a design problem, not a product problem, and an event or neighborhood campaign can teach a signal to a specific community in a way an app cannot.

## 10. Global precedents worth stealing

### Japan: dogs in carts, chartered bullet-train cars, and two kinds of dog cafe
- Trains: normally a pet must ride in a carrier no larger than 120 cm in combined length, width, and height and under 10 kg in total, with a 290 yen hand-luggage ticket, and may not occupy a seat. JR Central (the Tokaido Shinkansen operator) has run one-off chartered cars called the "Wan Wan Express" ("wan wan" is the Japanese "woof woof"):
  - 2025-03-09: one chartered car of a Tokyo to Shin-Osaka Nozomi train, 26 places, dogs out of their cages for the first time in the line's 60-year history, 59,000 yen for one owner. Dogs over 10 kg including cage were excluded.
  - 2026-06-20 and 21: two cars, up to 100 people, 35,800 to 83,300 yen per person bundled with a ticket to the Interpet Osaka pet trade show; dogs allowed on the seats for part of the trip; the operator tested seat covers and ran allergen removal and odor measurement to address passengers with allergies.
  - JR East ran a similar test from Ueno to Karuizawa in 2022. In a Yahoo Japan poll, 67.8% of respondents still preferred to travel with pets by car and 5.2% by train.
  - Sources: [Japan Times, 2025-01-06](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2025/01/06/companies/jr-central-shinkansen-pets/); [Nippon.com, 2025-01-03](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2025010300333/); [Travel Voice (Japanese), 2025-01-21](https://www.travelvoice.jp/20250121-157044); [JR Central press release PDF](https://jr-central.co.jp/news/release/_pdf/000044243.pdf); [JR Pass guide on pet rules](https://www.jrpass.com/blog/pets-on-japanese-trains-rules-for-dogs-cats-and-carriers). Confidence: high on 2025 run, moderate on 2026 details (Japanese-language sources summarized).
- Shopping: the common Japanese rule is "carrier, cart, or arms". Malls such as MOP Yokohama allow pets in participating shops if the pet is in a carrier bag or pet cart; Mitsui outlet parks mark dog-welcome shops with a window sticker ("no sticker, no entry"); LaLaport Toyosu has a "pet welcome area" with a dog run, pet shop, and dog cafe; department stores such as JR Nagoya Takashimaya admit only small dogs fully inside a carrier and never on food floors. Pet strollers are common enough that the brand Airbuggy reports 150,000+ pet-cart users, and pets in Japan outnumber children under 15 by more than 2 million (Al Jazeera, 2026-07-13).
  - Sources: [Mitsui Shopping Park MOP Yokohama pet rules](https://mitsui-shopping-park.com/en/mop/yokohama/info/pet.html); [BringFido Japan shops](https://www.bringfido.com/attraction/shops/country/japan/); [Al Jazeera, 2026-07-13](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/13/japans-pet-care-industry-booms-as-fur-babies-outnumber-infants). Confidence: moderate.
- Dog cafes come in two kinds: cafes with resident dogs for people who cannot keep a pet (Harajuku's cluster of animal cafes is nicknamed "Zoo Street"), and restaurants that welcome your own dog with dog menus and seat belts ([GoConnect, 2024-10-28](https://goconnect.jp/2024/10/28/dog-friendly-cafes-and-restaurants-tokyo-pet-friendly-cozy-dog-lovers/); [SJMC Japan](https://sjmcjapan.com/feature-story/exploring-the-vast-variety-of-tokyos-animal-cafes/)). Confidence: moderate.
- What to steal: (1) the cart or bag as a ticket into places, which is the Japanese answer to NYC's MTA bag rule; (2) the window sticker as a simple, shop-by-shop "dogs welcome here" signal; (3) the chartered event (a one-day "dog car") as a way to test a new service with 26 to 100 people before any permanent change.

### Tel Aviv: the self-declared dog capital
- The municipality called Tel Aviv the friendliest city for dogs, citing 1 dog per 17 residents, about 25,000 dogs, and 70 public dog parks (1.3 per square kilometer); a later city estimate was 30,000 dogs for 413,000 people. Dogs are allowed in most cafes and shops and on buses, trains, and taxi vans; the city shelter does not euthanize, and city vets treat homeless animals around the clock. The city launched a dog-owner app ("Digi-Dog") in 2017 and in 2021 planned DNA tests of dog waste to fine owners, after collecting about 500 kg from sidewalks a month.
- Comparison: the Times of Israel noted that NYC reported 600,000 dogs for 8.6 million people, a ratio within a fraction of a percent of Tel Aviv's (about 7.0 versus 7.3 dogs per 100 residents, my calculation from their figures). NYC has the dog density of the "dog capital" without its access rules.
  - Sources: [Times of Israel, "Tel Aviv claims to be city with most dogs per capita", about 2016](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/tel-aviv-claims-to-be-city-with-most-dogs-per-capita/); [Times of Israel, "Why Tel Aviv is so crazy about dogs"](https://www.timesofisrael.com/why-tel-aviv-is-so-crazy-about-dogs/); [Euronews, 2021-07-16](https://www.euronews.com/2021/07/16/tel-aviv-wants-to-use-dog-poo-dna-tests-to-catch-and-fine-careless-pet-owners); [The Travel Magazine on Digi-Dog](https://www.thetravelmagazine.net/tel-aviv-the-dog-friendliest-city-in-the-world-launches-digi-dog/). Confidence: moderate (city's own claims, mostly 2016 to 2017 figures).
- What to steal: the city treats dogs as residents with infrastructure (parks every few blocks, access to transit and cafes) and pairs it with enforcement (waste fines). Rights plus responsibilities, not just more amenities.

## White spaces nobody serves well

1. Safe, small-group, screened play for nervous or reactive dogs in Manhattan.
   - Evidence: Sniffspot doubled revenue to about $6 million in 2025 on this need, but its supply is backyards, which Manhattan barely has. Daycare chains turn away dogs that fail their tests. The only screened clubs cost $1,320 a year (DOG PPL, not yet open in Brooklyn) or $1,350 a month (Happy Tails).
2. A portable "this dog plays well" credential that travels with the dog.
   - Evidence: Dogtopia (20 to 30 minute Meet & Greet), Camp Bow Wow (3-hour interview), and D Is For Doggy ($42 assessment) each test separately, and results never leave the building. Dog bars check only vaccines, spay or neuter status, and a signed waiver (Bark Social, Fetch Park).
3. Dog time for dog-less New Yorkers, especially students, supplied by shelter dogs.
   - Evidence: BorrowMyDoggy shows borrowers will pay GBP 12.99 a year for access in the UK. ACC's BoroughBreak already lends shelter dogs for a day but launched at 5 dogs a day from one shelter. Columbia runs drop-in therapy-dog sessions, and medical students were "often already waiting" when the dogs arrived. A peer-reviewed study found a single outing makes a shelter dog 5 times more likely to be adopted.
4. Value before the network exists, through events.
   - Evidence: JR Central tested dogs on bullet trains with 26 people, then 100, as chartered one-day events. ACC runs "Love At First Wag" sessions for up to 10 guests. Permanent venues failed on financing and property (section 4). No one runs a recurring, curated, small-group dog event series for a specific NYC community such as a campus.
5. Teaching a whole neighborhood one shared signal or etiquette.
   - Evidence: the Yellow Dog Project exists in 45+ countries, yet no survey shows public recognition and owners report it is ignored. Japan uses window stickers to mark dog-welcome shops. NYC already requires "dogs allowed" signs at restaurants that allow dogs outside, so signs are a recognized tool.
6. Specific, verified "can my dog come in" details for daily NYC life.
   - Evidence: Google's attribute is a crowd-edited yes or no; NYC law limits restaurants to outdoor seating; the MTA requires a container. BringFido verifies policies by phone, but only for travel. Caution: this sits next to a crowded space; the white space is verification, not another list.
7. Supervised waiting while the owner runs an errand (dog parking), with regulatory risk.
   - Evidence: DogSpot was pulled from NYC sidewalks by DOT in 2018, and I found no sign that the 2019 pet-harbor pilot law was ever carried out. Sweden runs app-locked kennels at about 20 ICA stores on store property. In NYC this only works inside a partner's property with a person watching the dogs.

## Top 10 lessons for the project

1. Do not make a dog social or matching app the primary service. It is the most-tried and most-failed idea here (Dogster's community, Tindog, Dig, Petzbe), and the live ones are empty (DogHood averages about 45 users per city).
2. Attach social contact to a concrete job and a physical moment. The survivors (Rover, BorrowMyDoggy, TrustedHousesitters) sell care, walking, or a place to stay, and the relationship comes along with it.
3. Answer "empty at launch" with supply the team can control on day one: shelter dogs. NYC's shelter system already lends dogs out (BoroughBreak), and an outing makes a dog 5 times more likely to be adopted. One event with 10 shelter dogs and 30 students creates value with zero app users.
4. Charge for trust, not for access. BorrowMyDoggy, TrustedHousesitters, and Sniffspot all use memberships, and Sniffspot doubled revenue after it switched.
5. Financing and property kill dog venues. Bark Social went bankrupt, Fetch Park closed a site, a "premium" Alpharetta park lasted under 6 months, Boris & Horton nearly died, and DOG PPL Brooklyn keeps slipping. Borrow space through pop-ups, partner venues, and campus lawns.
6. Screening is the missing trust layer. Every daycare runs its own private test and nothing travels with the dog. If the project needs a digital tool, a portable "dog passport" earned at events is more defensible than a feed or a map.
7. Nervous and reactive dogs are an underserved, paying segment. Sniffspot grew on them, daycare chains turn them away, and the yellow-ribbon signal fails because nobody taught the public.
8. NYC rules shape every idea. DOT removes sidewalk boxes, the MTA requires a container, and restaurants may only allow dogs outdoors. Design inside these rules, or make a rule change the whimsical part of the pitch (Tel Aviv's dogs on buses, Japan's chartered dog train car).
9. Drop the all-in-one building. By my count 6 of its 8 elements already exist at PetSmart with a Banfield inside plus a Small Door or Bond Vet nearby, and the chains that own this format are closing stores.
10. Pilot with a time-boxed event before building anything. JR Central tested dogs on bullet trains with 26 riders, then 100. The midterm can propose a single-day pilot with a clear measure of success (for example, how many students return, how many dogs earn a "plays well" stamp, how many shelter dogs get adoption inquiries).
