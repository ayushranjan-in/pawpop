# Whimsical precedents for pets, and idea fuel tied to evidence

Date of research: 2026-10-20. Status: COMPLETE.

## The short answer

- **30 real precedents** are verified below, with sources, dates, and current status. 13 of them are in or near New York City, or fly from it.
- **The patterns that matter:** the dog as the customer or audience is the core delight move; borrowed human rituals (elections, funerals, passports) make pets civic; events create value before any network exists; services built on scarce assets or public sidewalk space broke down (Pet Airways collapsed, and DogSpot was ordered off New York sidewalks).
- **20 speculative concept seeds** for New York City follow, each tied to a real problem, a "Ben 10" fantasy version, and a version the team can stage with paper, a park, or a phone.
- **The five strongest seeds** are Speed-Sniffing (dog compatibility), the Quiet Hour Club (reactive dogs), the Pack Board and Walk Pool (trusted neighbor care), the "Is This Normal?" Desk (health and food confusion), and Dog Mayor of the Block (neighbors learning each other's names).
- **Recommendation (moderate confidence):** Prove one service first, "the right company for your dog" (Speed-Sniffing, with the Quiet Hour Club for dogs that want no company), and use the dog mayor election as the launch event.

## How to read this report

- **Real** means a source URL proves the thing exists or existed. Each real example lists its status as of the source date.
- **Speculative** means an idea from this report. It is never presented as an existing product.
- **Confidence levels:** high (primary or several independent sources agree), moderate (one good source, or details unclear), low (thin or old evidence).

### 1.1 BARK Air, an airline where the dog is the main passenger (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** A charter flight service run by BARK, the company behind the BarkBox toy subscription. Dogs of any size sit in the cabin with their owner, with no crate. Flights are "shared charters", which means a flight only takes off once enough seats sell.
- **Proof:** BARK's own press release, "BARK Air Celebrates First Anniversary with New Routes and Private Charters", BusinessWire, 2025-05-14: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250514211437/en/BARK-Air-Celebrates-First-Anniversary-with-New-Routes-and-Private-Charters . It reports more than 1,000 dogs flown on 142 flights in the first year, including free evacuation flights for Los Angeles County shelter dogs during the wildfires. Booking page: https://air.bark.co/collections/bookings
- **Status (2026-10-20):** Operating. The booking page lists New York to Madrid flights on 2026-11-20 and 2026-12-13 at $11,800 for one dog plus one human, and a Madrid to New York flight on 2026-12-14 marked "Awaiting Minimum Bookings To Operate Flight". Travel + Leisure (via Yahoo, early 2026) reported four new 2026 European routes (Dublin, Stockholm, Athens, Berlin) from $7,850: https://travel.yahoo.com/advice/lifestyle/articles/bark-air-adding-4-european-125326517.html
- **Need it serves:** Big dogs cannot fly in a normal cabin and owners fear the cargo hold. International moves with a dog are stressful.
- **Delight mechanism:** The roles reverse. The dog is the customer and the human is the plus-one. Calming touches (pheromone towels, a dog "champagne", bone broth) are designed around the dog.
- **Lesson for the team:** The fantasy works, but only at luxury prices and only when the plane fills. It shows the emotional pull of "the dog comes first", but it does not show a mass-market model.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists and flies; moderate on the exact 2026 routes and prices, which change often.

### 1.2 Pet Airways, the pet-only airline that failed (REAL, defunct)

- **What it was:** An airline that flew pets without their owners in the main cabin of leased Beech 1900 planes, from 2009 (first flight 2009-07-16). Fares started around $150 per pet. It flew about 9,000 animals, from dogs and cats to a parrot and a potbellied pig.
- **Proof:** The Boston Globe, "Humans howl as Pet Airways cancels flights", 2012-02-12: https://www.bostonglobe.com/business/2012/02/12/humans-howl-pet-airways-cancels-flights/rJ1h3caIp9OCXET6ZK2lRN/story.html . Wikipedia summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Airways
- **Status:** Stopped flying around late 2011 and early 2012. The company did not own planes, lost its plane supplier, cancelled flights, and customers complained about missing refunds. Later restart plans never produced flights, as far as any source shows.
- **Lesson for the team:** A delightful service that depends on scarce infrastructure (planes) and a full network collapses when one link breaks. Pet Airways needed planes and full flights before it could deliver anything, which is why the professor's question ("what creates value before the network is large?") matters.
- **Confidence:** high that it existed and failed; moderate on the exact end date.

### 1.3 Laurie Anderson's "Concert for Dogs" in Times Square (REAL, one-time event in New York City)

- **What it was:** A late-night concert played for dogs. Dogs sat on the red steps of Duffy Square (Broadway between 46th and 47th Streets) close to low-volume speakers. Their humans listened through wireless "silent disco" headphones. It ended with the dogs barking together.
- **Proof:** Times Square Arts project page: https://www.timessquarenyc.org/tsq-arts-projects/heart-of-a-dog-concert-for-dogs . Gothamist photos and videos: https://gothamist.com/arts-entertainment/photos-videos-from-laurie-andersons-captivating-times-square-concert-for-dogs . CBS New York: https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/laurie-anderson-dog-concert-times-square/
- **Date and status:** 2016-01-04, about 11:30 pm, in freezing weather. It opened a month of "Midnight Moment" screenings of her film "Heart of a Dog", which mourns her rat terrier Lolabelle and her husband Lou Reed. She played an earlier dog concert at the Sydney Opera House in 2010. A video is on the Criterion Channel: https://www.criterionchannel.com/videos/concert-for-dogs
- **Need it serves:** A shared experience for owner and dog in a city that rarely designs anything for dogs. Grief and love for a dog, in public.
- **Delight mechanism:** The dog is the audience and the human is the guest. Hundreds of strangers gathered near midnight for one absurd, tender reason.
- **Lesson for the team:** It took one night, one place, and existing hardware (headphones and speakers), with no network needed. This is the kind of "event first, digital support second" model the professor suggested.
- **Confidence:** high.

- **Proof:** Official site: http://www.dogfilmfestival.com/festival/dog-film-festival-program . 2026 tour stop at Circle Cinema, Tulsa (2026-05-29 to 2026-05-31): https://www.circlecinema.org/movies-events/dog-film-festival-2026 . Gateway Film Center listing: https://gatewayfilmcenter.org/movies/2026-ny-dog-film-festival/
- **Status (2026):** Active. The 10th program toured during 2026. Search results report the 11th edition premiering in New York City on 2026-09-27 at the Village East theater, with the NY Cat Film Festival the day before benefiting Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC, the city's public shelter system).
- **Need it serves:** Community among dog lovers, money for shelters, and dog joy for people who cannot own a dog.
- **Delight mechanism:** For two hours, the whole cinema shares one obsession.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists and tours; moderate on the 2026 New York date and venue.

### 1.5 Dogs elected as town mayors (REAL, ongoing)

- **What it is:** Rabbit Hash, Kentucky, a tiny unincorporated river town, has elected a dog as mayor since 1998. Every vote costs $1, people can vote as many times as they like, and the money goes to the Rabbit Hash Historical Society.
- **Proof:** Spectrum News 1, "Sixth dog mayor is inaugurated in Rabbit Hash", 2025-01-12: https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/human-interest/2025/01/12/mayor-boone . FOX56: https://fox56news.com/news/kentucky/meet-boone-the-bluetick-coonhound-elected-mayor-of-rabbit-hash/
- **Status:** The current mayor is Boone, a Bluetick Coonhound, who won the 2024-11-05 election with 14,162 votes against 10 dogs and one robot. The election raised over $26,000, with votes from Japan, Denmark, Germany, and Australia. His term runs to the next election in 2028. Earlier mayors included Goofy (1998), Lucy Lou, Brynneth Pawltro, and Wilbur the French bulldog.
- **Need it serves:** Fundraising for a small community, a shared town identity, and a reason for neighbors to talk.
- **Delight mechanism:** The election parodies democracy, with real money behind it. Campaign slogans ("Have a drink at noon and vote for Boone") and inaugurations turn a dog into a civic character.
- **Lesson for the team:** A block, a building, or a dog run could elect its own dog mayor. The fundraising mechanic (pay to vote) is proven.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.6 "Architecture for Dogs" by Kenya Hara (REAL, archive and touring exhibition)

- **What it is:** Japanese designer Kenya Hara asked famous architects (Kengo Kuma, Shigeru Ban, Toyo Ito, Kazuyo Sejima, Sou Fujimoto, MVRDV, and others) to design a small structure for one specific dog breed each. The blueprints are free to download so owners can build them.
- **Proof:** CNN, 2012-12-20: https://www.cnn.com/2012/12/20/world/asia/dog-kennel-architecture-kenya-hara/index.html . Designboom on the Milan show, 2024-12-02: https://www.designboom.com/design/architecture-for-dogs-exhibition-adi-design-museum-mvrdv-kenya-hara-12-02-2024/ . Hara's studio page: https://ndc.co.jp/hara/en/works/2014/08/architecturefordogs.html
- **Status:** Launched 2012-11-15 at Design Miami. Shown at Japan House London (2020) and the ADI Design Museum in Milan (2024-12-03 to 2025-02-16). No later showing found.
- **Need it serves:** Dogs live at human scale in human homes. Owners want to express care through objects.
- **Delight mechanism:** A serious architect treats a pug or a poodle as the client. Hara called his own piece a "scale modifier" that puts human and dog at the same scale.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.7 DOGTV, television made for dogs home alone (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** A streaming channel founded in 2012 with programs sorted into "stimulation", "relaxation", and "exposure" (getting used to sounds like doorbells and car rides), built around how dogs see and hear.
- **Proof:** Company site: https://www.dogtv.com/happier-dogs/ . Google Play listing (updated 2026-09-24): https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.latto.tv.dogtv&hl=en_US . DISH add-on: https://www.dish.com/add-ons/lifestyle/dogtv
- **Status:** Operating in 2026.
- **Need it serves:** The dog left alone during work and errands, and the owner's guilt about it.
- **Delight mechanism:** The dog is the viewer.
- **Caution:** The company's claim that 85% of subscribers see a calmer dog comes from its own survey, not independent research.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists; low that it measurably helps dogs.

### 1.8 Talking buttons for dogs, and the research behind them (REAL, research ongoing)

- **What it is:** Owners teach dogs to press recordable buttons that say words such as "outside" or "play". The trend went viral through Bunny, a sheepadoodle owned by Alexis Devine. FluentPet sells the button kits.
- **Proof:** Bastos, Rossano and colleagues, "How do soundboard-trained dogs respond to human button presses? An investigation into word comprehension", PLOS ONE, 2024, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0307189. UC San Diego news release: https://today.ucsd.edu/story/dogs-understand-words-from-soundboard-buttons-study-reveals
- **Finding:** In tests with 30 dogs at home plus 29 owners running trials remotely, dogs responded correctly to "play" and "outside" whether a button or a person said the word, and whether the owner or a stranger pressed it. Dogs were seven times more likely to play after the "play" button.
- **Limit:** The study tested whether dogs understand the buttons, not whether dogs "talk" with them. Studies of how dogs use the buttons are still under way.
- **Need it serves:** Owners want to know what their dog wants, and they want a closer bond.
- **Delight mechanism:** The dog "speaks" in the owner's own recorded voice.
- **Confidence:** high on the study; moderate on the bigger claims made online about "talking" dogs.

### 1.9 AI that decodes barks (REAL research; consumer "translators" are mostly novelty)

- **What it is:** University of Michigan researchers with Mexico's National Institute of Astrophysics, Optics and Electronics (INAOE) adapted an AI speech model trained on human speech (Wav2Vec2) to classify dog barks. It told playful barks from aggressive ones and also guessed breed, age, and sex.
- **Proof:** University of Michigan release via ScienceDaily, 2024-06-04: https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/06/240604132204.htm . Paper: Abzaliev, Perez Espinosa, Mihalcea, "Towards Dog Bark Decoding: Leveraging Human Speech Processing for Automated Bark Classification", arXiv 2404.18739, presented at the LREC-COLING 2024 conference.
- **Status:** Academic research on recordings of 74 dogs. Not a product that translates barks into sentences.
- **Need it serves:** Reading a dog's state, especially for owners of reactive or anxious dogs who worry about encounters.
- **Delight mechanism:** It feeds the "Doctor Dolittle" fantasy of talking with animals.
- **Confidence:** high that the research exists; low for any claim that an app can truly "translate" a bark.

### 1.10 Dog-friendly trains in Japan (REAL, one-off trips and a new bus)

- **What it is:** Japanese railways normally require pets to ride in a carrier weighing at most 10 kg in total. JR East (East Japan Railway Company) has run special trips where dogs ride out of the carrier.
- **Proof and dates:**
  - May 2022: a chartered "shinkansen for pets" (bullet train) pilot carried 21 dogs and owners from Ueno, Tokyo, to Karuizawa, with dogs allowed out of their cages for 40 minutes on laps or covered seats. Jakarta Post, 2022-05-22: https://www.thejakartapost.com/culture/2022/05/22/paw-some-day-trip-dogs-ride-japans-bullet-train.html
  - December 2023: a special Tokyo to Izu train let small dogs ride without cages, sold as a package with dog-friendly hotels. SoraNews24, 2023-10-18: https://soranews24.com/2023/10/18/japan-railway-to-allow-dogs-to-ride-without-cages-on-special-tokyo-izu-train/
  - August 2026: a dog-friendly "Wanwan Liner" bus between Tokyo and Osaka was announced for fall 2026, with longer rest stops at highway dog runs. "Wan" is the Japanese sound for a bark. SoraNews24, 2026-08-19: https://soranews24.com/2026/08/19/dog-friendly-wanwan-liner-bus-to-begin-operations-between-tokyo-and-osaka-this-fall/
- **Status:** No regular service. Special trips only, plus the new bus (start not confirmed).
- **Need it serves:** Getting around with a dog, especially a dog too big for a carrier. This mirrors the New York subway rule (see 1.21).
- **Delight mechanism:** A whole train car becomes a dog space, sold as an occasion rather than a commute.
- **Confidence:** high for the 2022 and 2023 trips; moderate for the bus details.

### 1.11 Hotel dogs that guests can borrow: Fairmont's "Canine Ambassadors" (REAL, long-running)

- **What it is:** Resident hotel dogs that greet guests in the lobby and can be booked for walks or runs. Guests do not need to bring a pet. The first was Morgan, a black Labrador, at the Fairmont Waterfront in Vancouver in 2002.

- **Need it serves:** Travelers who miss their own dog, and people who cannot own a dog.

- **Confidence:** high for the program; moderate for which hotels run it today.

### 1.12 Therapy dogs at airports: LAX's PUP program (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** "Pets Unstressing Passengers" (PUP) at Los Angeles International Airport. Volunteer handlers walk their own registered therapy dogs, in red "Pet Me" vests, through departure gate areas to calm travelers. It started on 2013-04-15 with 30 dogs and partners with Alliance of Therapy Dogs.
- **Proof:** Los Angeles World Airports program pages: https://www.lawa.org/groups-and-divisions/operations-and-emergency-management/guest-services/pups and https://www.lawa.org/volunteer-opportunities/pup-program . - **Confidence:** high.

### 1.13 Shelter dog "day outs" in New York City: ACC BoroughBreaks and Straycations (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** Animal Care Centers of NYC (ACC, the city's open-admission public shelter system) lets any adult take a large shelter dog out for one hour up to a full day: a walk, a beach trip, or a nap on a couch. Dogs wear an "adopt me" bandana. "Straycations" let people host a dog at home for a few days after an online orientation.
- **Proof:** ACC program pages: https://www.nycacc.org/boroughbreak/ and https://www.nycacc.org/straycations/ . ACC's guidelines, published by ASPCApro (2024): https://www.aspcapro.org/sites/default/files/2024-08/acc_boroughbreak_guidelines.pdf . NY1 on the launch, 2019-07-30: https://ny1.com/nyc/bronx/news/2019/07/30/dog-adoptions-new-york-city-nyc-acc-borough-break-program-people-hang-out-with-dogs
- **Status:** Operating at the Manhattan, Queens, and Staten Island centers. Weekly slots fill fast. In its first months (2019) about 100 dogs went out and about a dozen were adopted as a direct result.
- **Rules that matter for any design:** The dog stays on leash, stays out of dog parks, and must not meet other dogs.
- **Related programs:** VCA Charities runs a national "Dog Day Out" field trip program (launched 2023) that lists ACC as a partner: https://www.vcacharities.org/articles/DogDayOut . Best Friends Animal Society runs an "Adventure Buddy" outing program at its SoHo center (named in search results; not separately checked).
- **Need it serves:** Kennel stress for shelter dogs. Dog time for New Yorkers who cannot own a dog. Adoption visibility.
- **Delight mechanism:** People get a "date" with a dog. The day itself is the reward, and an adoption can follow.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.14 Pet funerals near New York City: Hartsdale Pet Cemetery and the "buried together" laws (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** The Hartsdale Pet Cemetery in Westchester, founded in 1896 when a veterinarian let a client bury her dog in his apple orchard. It holds more than 80,000 animals and, by its own count, over 800 people whose ashes rest with their pets.
- **Proof:** Cemetery site: https://petcem.com/ . Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsdale_Pet_Cemetery . ABC7 New York: https://abc7ny.com/post/see-the-famous-pet-cemetery-where-celebrity-pets-are-buried/2553212/ . It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2012.
- **Law:** In September 2016 Governor Andrew Cuomo signed a law letting New Yorkers be buried with their cremated pets in not-for-profit cemeteries that agree (NY1, 2016-09-26): https://www.ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2016/09/26/cuomo-signs-law-that-allows-pets-to-be-buried-with-their-owners . Human ashes in pet cemeteries were briefly halted by the state in 2011, then allowed again under later rules (sources disagree on 2012 or 2014).
- **Need it serves:** Pet loss, and the wish to stay together after death.
- **Delight mechanism:** The pull here is meaning rather than delight. The ritual makes grief for a pet legitimate and public.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.15 Buddhist funerals for robot dogs in Japan (REAL, last confirmed 2018)

- **What it is:** At Kofuku-ji temple in Isumi, Chiba Prefecture, priests held funerals for broken Sony Aibo robot dogs before a repair company (A-Fun) took them apart for spare parts. Owners sent letters with their dogs.
- **Proof:** NPR, 2018-05-01: https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2018/05/01/607295346/in-japan-old-robot-dogs-get-a-buddhist-send-off . National Geographic: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/in-japan--a-buddhist-funeral-service-for-robot-dogs
- **Status:** The first funeral (2015) honored 17 Aibos. By April 2018 about 800 had been honored. No source found after 2019, so current status is unknown.
- **Need it serves:** Grief for a companion, even a machine one. The priest said, "All things have a bit of soul."
- **Delight mechanism:** A serious ritual is applied to something people expect to throw away. It suggests that the ritual itself carries the meaning, whatever the object.
- **Confidence:** high for 2015 to 2018; unknown for today.

### 1.16 Dog birthdays, weddings, and "bark mitzvahs" (REAL, a recurring trend)

- **What it is:** Owners throw ceremonies for dogs, often at 13 years or 13 months old, with kippahs (Jewish skullcaps), cakes, and guests. Many of the best-known cases are in the New York area.
- **Proof:** Yahoo Lifestyle, "Some dogs just turn 13. Others get a 'bark mitzvah.'" (spring 2026): https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/pets-animals/article/some-dogs-just-turn-13-others-get-a-bark-mitzvah-090052247.html . - **Confidence:** high that it happens; moderate on its origin story.

### 1.17 City design rules for pets: Toronto's Pet Friendly Design Guidelines (REAL, in force as guidance)

- **What it is:** City of Toronto guidelines (2019) for new high-rise buildings: dog relief areas indoors and outdoors, pet wash stations, pet-friendly lobbies and elevators, and a recommended pet share of at least 10% of a building's amenity space.
- **Proof:** City page: https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/planning-studies-initiatives/pet-friendly-design-guidelines-for-high-density-communities/ . The PDF: https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/94d3-CityPlanning-Pet-Friendly-Guidelines.pdf
- **Why it exists:** The city estimated 4 to 8 pets per floor of a new high-rise, while public park space was not growing. One condo parkette had to be resodded every year because of dog urine.
- **Status:** Still listed among the city's design guidelines. The guidelines are advisory, not mandatory.
- **Need it serves:** Dogs in dense vertical cities, which is New York's situation.
- **Delight mechanism:** The guidelines have no delight of their own, but they show a city treating dogs as residents. One consultation was a "Pet-Friendly Pop-up" held in Berczy Park on 2018-05-10.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.18 Sensory and "sniff" gardens for dogs, including one in New York City (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** Gardens built for a dog's nose: herbs at different heights, varied ground surfaces, tunnels, digging pits, and water.
- **Proof:** Dogs Trust Harefield (United Kingdom) sensory garden, opened late 2010: https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/rehoming/our-centres/harefield/sensory-garden . ASPCApro on the ASPCA Adoption Center in New York City, whose "Sniff 'n' Search Meadow" serves dogs that are fearful or over-aroused on walks: https://www.aspcapro.org/resource/building-sensory-garden-shelter-dogs-enhancing-well-being-through-stimulating-spaces
- **Status:** Operating at shelters. No public sniff garden for owned dogs in New York City was found in this research.
- **Need it serves:** Sniffing is how dogs take in the world. Reactive and fearful dogs need low-stress spaces without other dogs.
- **Delight mechanism:** The space is designed for the dog's main sense, which human city design ignores.
- **Confidence:** high that these exist; the claim that no public New York sniff garden exists is low confidence (absence of evidence).

### 1.19 Public art for dogs: the Berczy Park dog fountain, Toronto (REAL, operating since 2017)

- **What it is:** A two-tier cast-iron fountain with 27 life-size dog statues spouting water while staring at a golden bone on top. One cat statue ignores the bone and watches two birds on a lamp post. A trough at ground level lets real dogs drink.
- **Proof:** Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berczy_Park . The Cultural Landscape Foundation: https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/berczy-park . Landezine on Claude Cormier's design: https://landezine.com/berczy-park-toronto-by-claude-cormier/
- **Status:** The redesigned park reopened on 2017-06-28 and the fountain is a well-known landmark.
- **Why it was designed this way:** About 400 children lived within two blocks, and dogs visited more than 1,500 times a day, so families, businesses, and dog owners fought over the small park. The fountain was meant to tie three zones (lawn, dog garden, plaza) together.
- **Delight mechanism:** The fountain is a joke in cast iron that both dogs and humans use. Real dogs drink at the feet of statue dogs.
- **Lesson for the team:** Whimsy defused a real conflict between dog owners and non-owners.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.20 The Tompkins Square Halloween Dog Parade, East Village, New York City (REAL, 36th year in 2026)

- **What it is:** A costume parade and contest for dogs, held each October since 1990.
- **Proof:** amNY: https://www.amny.com/lifestyle/holidays/tompkins-square-halloween-dog-parade-east-village-36th-year/ . Forbes, 2026-09-22: https://www.forbes.com/sites/melissakravitz/2026/09/22/tompkins-square-dog-parade-returns-for-36th-year-in-the-east-village/ . EV Grieve, October 2026: https://evgrieve.com/2026/10/fyi-tompkins-square-halloween-dog.html . Organizer: https://www.nydogparade.org/nydpparades
- **Status:** The 36th parade ran on Saturday 2026-10-17 along Avenue B, run by the nonprofit New York Dog Parade with a pet food sponsor. Reports say about 500 dogs marched and organizers brought 250 adoptable shelter dogs. The 2023 parade was announced as cancelled, then saved after public backlash. Some locals complain it has become a corporate social media event.
- **Need it serves:** Neighborhood belonging, shelter adoptions, and a reason for strangers to talk.
- **Delight mechanism:** Costumes, a crowd, and a prize turn a walk into a show.
- **Lesson for the team:** New York already proves that a dog event can draw hundreds of owners without any app. The risk is that sponsors take over and locals feel pushed out.
- **Confidence:** high for the parade; moderate for the 2026 attendance numbers.

### 1.21 The New York subway "dog in a bag" rule and the IKEA bag workaround (REAL, everyday New York behavior)

- **What it is:** The Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) rules of conduct say no one may bring an animal into the subway "unless enclosed in a container". Service animals are exempt. The rule sets no size limit, so owners of big dogs carry them in giant shopping bags, often the IKEA FRAKTA bag with leg holes cut out.
- **Proof:** Inside Edition, "How Big Dog Owners Bring Their Pups on New York City's Subway": https://www.insideedition.com/dog-owners-find-out-of-the-box-ways-to-bring-their-pups-on-new-york-citys-subway-80765 . Guide summarizing the rule (usually cited as section 1050.9(h)): https://dogcityguide.com/articles/dogs-on-nyc-subway-rules/
- **Status:** The rule stands in 2026. Enforcement is loose and depends on staff.
- **Need it serves:** Big dogs are shut out of the main way New Yorkers get around. This matches the interview finding that big dogs are shut out of "dog-friendly" places.
- **Delight mechanism:** Citizens comply with an absurd rule in an absurd way, and the photos go viral. The workaround is already a piece of New York folklore.
- **Confidence:** high for the rule and the behavior; moderate on the exact section number.

### 1.22 "Dog parking" boxes outside New York shops: DogSpot, formerly Dog Parker (REAL, pushed off New York sidewalks)

- **What it is:** A Brooklyn startup (founded 2015) put locked, heated, air-conditioned dog houses with webcams outside shops, so owners could run errands where dogs are not allowed. Owners unlocked a box by phone and paid by the minute (about 30 cents) or used it free where a sponsor paid.
- **Proof:** Brooklyn Eagle, "Dog Gone: Why did the city kick out the digital dog houses?": https://brooklyneagle.com/65930/dog-gone-why-did-the-city-kick-out-the-digital-dog-houses/ . Patch on the Department of Transportation order: https://patch.com/new-york/bed-stuy/high-tech-dog-houses-must-go-dot-tells-brooklyn-business . Patch on the City Council "pet harbors" pilot bill: https://patch.com/new-york/fortgreene/pet-harbors-can-be-put-outside-brooklyn-shops-council-says . Microsoft feature on the national expansion: https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/digital-transformation/retail-smart-doghouses-sniffing-out-new-fans-across-country/
- **Status:** In November 2017 the city Department of Transportation ordered all units (more than 40 in Brooklyn, with more than 1,000 customer accounts) off public sidewalks. The City Council later passed a bill for a "pet harbor" pilot (around 2019). The company expanded to 14 states. This research did not confirm whether any units operate in New York City in 2026.
- **Need it serves:** Exactly the interview finding of "drop-off spots across the city" for errands. Health rules keep dogs out of grocery stores.
- **Lesson for the team:** The need is proven, but a physical box on public sidewalk ran into city rules. A drop-off service needs a host (a shop, a lobby, a doorman) on private space.
- **Confidence:** high for the history; unknown for current New York operation.

### 1.23 Volunteer help for older and ill owners in New York City: PAWS NY (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** Pets Are Wonderful Support (PAWS NY), a nonprofit founded in 2008, sends trained volunteers to walk dogs, clean litter boxes, and give medication for older adults and people with illness or disability, so they can keep their pets. It also runs a foster network for when owners are hospitalized.
- **Proof:** PAWS NY Housecall Program: https://pawsny.org/what-we-do/ . West Side Rag, 2024-02-03: https://www.westsiderag.com/2024/02/03/paws-volunteers-allow-people-with-barriers-to-keep-their-pets . Press release on its 5,000th volunteer (November 2023): https://www.pr.com/press-release/900267
- **Status:** Operating. By November 2023 it had trained 5,000 volunteers and helped more than 1,500 older adults and people with illness or disability since 2009. Volunteers commit to one weekly shift at the same day and time.
- **Need it serves:** Directly the interview finding that elderly owners cannot walk their dogs.
- **Delight mechanism:** The service is practical rather than whimsical. One client told West Side Rag, "Because of them, we can keep Clover."
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.24 Borrowing a neighbor's dog: BorrowMyDoggy (REAL, United Kingdom and Ireland)

- **What it is:** A website and app that matches dog owners with local "borrowers" who walk or mind the dog for free, for the love of it. Members pay a yearly fee to message each other.
- **Proof:** How it works: https://www.borrowmydoggy.com/how-it-works . Membership prices (£48.99 a year for owners, £12.99 for borrowers): https://www.borrowmydoggy.com/membership . Founder Rikke Rosenlund stated over 1 million members in the UK and Ireland: https://www.leapventurestudio.com/borrowmydoggy-puts-all-paws-in-caring-hands/
- **Status:** Operating in 2026. A co-founder's blog reports that some city postcodes had 100 borrowers for every owner, while the countryside had the reverse problem: https://www.oldtinroof.com/posts/what-dog-borrowing-taught-me-about-two-sided-marketplaces/
- **Need it serves:** People who cannot own a dog (renters, travelers, students) want dog time. Owners need help during work and travel.
- **Lesson for the team:** In dense cities, people who want to borrow dogs far outnumber dogs. For New York, the scarce side is the owner who trusts a stranger with the dog. That links to the interview finding that people trust "people who know the dog" over star ratings.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists; moderate on the member count, which mixes free and paid accounts.

### 1.25 The EU pet passport, the real base for any "dog citizenship" idea (REAL, official)

- **What it is:** An official booklet, issued by an authorized vet in the European Union, that lets a dog, cat, or ferret travel between member countries. It records the microchip number, the rabies vaccination, and, where required, a tapeworm treatment.
- **Proof:** European Commission, "Travelling with a pet within the EU": https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/live-animal-movements/dogs-cats-and-ferrets/travelling-pet-within-eu_en
- **Rules that matter (checked on the Commission page on 2026-10-20):** The microchip must be implanted before the rabies vaccine, because a vaccine given before the chip does not count (Canadian Food Inspection Agency guidance warns about this order). The pet must be at least 12 weeks old on the day of vaccination. The vaccination becomes valid "not less than 21 days" after the vaccination course ends. The passport model is now set by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705. Older passports stay valid: the 2003 model if issued before 2014-12-29, the 2013 model if issued before 2026-04-22, and transitional passports on the 2013 model if issued before 2028-01-01.
- **"Dog citizenship" rituals:** This research found no real government ceremony that makes a dog a citizen. The passport is the closest real thing. New York City law requires every dog to be licensed and to wear the tag (see the evidence table below), which is a second real base.
- **Need it serves:** The interview finding about international travel paperwork for pets.
- **Delight mechanism (latent):** A passport is a proud object for humans, with stamps and a photo. For pets it is a dry vet form. That gap is open design space.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.26 Renting a private yard for a reactive dog: Sniffspot (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** A marketplace where people rent out their fenced yards or land by the hour as private dog parks. Owners of reactive dogs (dogs that bark, lunge, or panic around other dogs or people) use it because every booking is private and listings can be filtered for "no dogs audible or visible nearby".
- **Proof:** Company site: https://www.sniffspot.com/ . Apple App Store listing: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sniffspot-private-dog-parks/id1437699295 . HumanePro magazine, "Your own private dog park": https://humanepro.org/magazine/articles/your-own-private-dog-park
- **Status:** Operating across the United States and Canada with thousands of listings. Prices are now shown in "credits"; the dollar value was not confirmed in this research.
- **Need it serves:** The interview finding about sensitive dogs who want no dog friends. Public dog runs fail these dogs.
- **Lesson for the team:** In New York City, private yards are rare. The equivalent scarce space is an empty courtyard, a rooftop, a closed shop, or a dog run at an off-peak hour.
- **Confidence:** high.

### 1.27 New York's dog cafe: Boris & Horton (REAL, closed and reopened)

- **What it is:** New York City's first dog cafe, opened around 2018 on Avenue A at 12th Street in the East Village, with a dog-friendly room separated from the food counter (New York health rules keep dogs out of food-prep areas).
- **Proof:** CBS New York on the planned closure (February 2024): https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/beloved-dog-cafe-boris-horton-closing-both-locations/ . Greenpointers, 2024-05-21: https://greenpointers.com/2024/05/21/after-a-successful-crowdfunding-campaign-boris-and-horton-announces-its-officially-closing/ . Time Out on the new owner (January 2025): https://timeout.com/newyork/news/dog-cafe-boris-horton-is-now-under-new-ownership-and-will-have-a-grand-reopening-to-celebrate-011725 . EV Grieve on the reopening (April 2025): https://evgrieve.com/2025/04/first-look-at-revamped-dog-cafe-boris.html
- **Status:** The owners announced closure in February 2024, raised about $250,000 by crowdfunding, closed the Williamsburg branch in May 2024, and closed the East Village cafe in November 2024 after reporting almost 90% churn (cancellations) in monthly memberships. A former customer, Carol Krakowski, bought it and reopened it on 2025-04-12. Listings in 2026 show it open, with a $5 fee for the dog side that members do not pay.
- **Need it serves:** A warm indoor place for dogs and owners in bad weather, and a social room where owners meet.
- **Lesson for the team:** A beloved place nearly died because a monthly membership did not hold. Affection alone did not keep members paying, so a service needs a recurring reason for people to come back.
- **Confidence:** high for the history; moderate for the 2026 status (based on listings, not news).

### 1.28 A yellow ribbon that means "give us space": the Yellow Dog Project and Gulahund (REAL, volunteer movement)

- **What it is:** Owners tie a yellow ribbon or bandana on a dog's leash to tell strangers that the dog needs space. The reason can be fear, recovery from surgery, or training. The signal began with U.S. trainer Terry Ryan, spread through Australian dog clubs, and became a public campaign in Sweden ("Gulahund", launched 2012-06-27) and Canada (The Yellow Dog Project, Red Deer, Alberta, September 2012).
- **Proof:** Gulahund history page: http://gulahund.se/history/ . Yellow Dog UK: https://yellowdoguk.co.uk/about-us/ . Pet Professional Guild Australia: https://ppgaustralia.net.au/Owners/Advice/Dogs/Yellow-Dog-Project
- **Status:** Active as an awareness signal run by several separate groups. It has no legal force. Gulahund says the ribbon never replaces a muzzle on a dog that bites, and it advises against yellow vests because they look like assistance-dog vests.
- **Weakness:** The signal only works if strangers know what it means. A 2014 blog post titled "Live in the U.S.? DON'T Tie a Yellow Ribbon on Your Dog's Leash" warned U.S. owners against it (its reasoning was not checked in this research): https://debmcalister.com/2014/01/05/dont-tie-a-yellow-ribbon-on-your-dogs-list/ . This research found no data on how many Americans recognize the ribbon.
- **Need it serves:** The interview finding about sensitive dogs who want no dog friends.
- **Delight mechanism:** A tiny, cheap, visible code turns a private worry into a public request.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists; unknown whether New Yorkers recognize it.

### 1.29 Supervised shelter playgroups that sort dogs by play style: Dogs Playing for Life (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** A Colorado nonprofit founded by trainer Aimee Sadler that teaches shelters to run daily supervised playgroups. Staff watch how each dog plays, which helps them judge sociability and match adopters. The idea started at the Southampton Animal Shelter in Hampton Bays, New York.
- **Proof:** About page: https://dogsplayingforlife.com/about-dogs-playing-for-life/ . Best Friends Animal Society feature: https://bestfriends.org/stories/features/dog-playgroups-increase-adoptablity-shelter-dogs
- **Status:** A nonprofit since March 2015. Its YouTube description says the program has reached more than 170 shelters in the United States and Canada (possibly out of date).
- **Claims to treat with care:** Sadler says many shelter dogs are wrongly labeled dog-aggressive. The program says shelters using it fully keep live-release rates above 95%. Both are the program's own claims.
- **Need it serves:** The interview finding about worry over which dogs get along. Playgroups show that compatibility can be observed in a structured way, not guessed.
- **Delight mechanism:** People get to watch dogs choose their own friends.
- **Confidence:** high that it exists; moderate for its outcome claims.

### 1.30 New York's cat cafe: Meow Parlour (REAL, operating)

- **What it is:** New York City's first permanent cat cafe, opened in December 2014 on the Lower East Side. Visitors book time with adoptable cats. Since 2018 it has run a licensed rescue, Meow Parlour Cats, which says it has fostered more than 3,000 cats.
- **Proof:** Official site: https://www.meowparlour.com/about . A cat cafe guide from October 2026 lists it among four cat cafes open in New York City in 2026 and the only one left in Manhattan: https://catcafemap.com/blog/cat-cafes-in-nyc
- **Status:** Open at 43 Essex Street.
- **Need it serves:** Cat time for people who cannot own a cat, and adoption for rescue cats.
- **Delight mechanism:** Visitors pay for time with animals they do not own, and adoption is an optional ending.
- **Confidence:** high.

### Patterns across the real examples

1. **The dog as the customer or the audience is the core delight move.** BARK Air, the Concert for Dogs, DOGTV, and Architecture for Dogs all flip the usual order, and the human becomes the guest.
2. **Borrowed human rituals make pets civic and meaningful.** Mayoral elections, bark mitzvahs, funerals, and passports take a human rite and hand it to an animal.
3. **Events work before a network exists.** The Concert for Dogs, the Tompkins Square parade, the film festival, and shelter playgroups all created value on day one with no user base.
4. **Scarce physical assets and public space break services.** Pet Airways collapsed after its plane supply failed and it could not raise money. DogSpot was ordered off the sidewalk. Boris & Horton nearly died when about 90% of monthly members cancelled.
5. **Trust runs through the same people over time.** PAWS NY volunteers keep a fixed weekly shift, the Fairmont hotel dog has a fixed handler, and ACC staff match each person with a dog.
6. **In dense cities, dog lovers outnumber dogs.** BorrowMyDoggy saw 100 borrowers per owner in some city areas. Volunteers and borrowers are plentiful, and the scarce side is owners willing to trust them.
7. **Space is New York's hidden limit.** The subway container rule, the NYCHA 25-pound limit, the Berczy Park fight over a one-acre park, Toronto's need for pet design rules, and Sniffspot's reliance on private yards all point to space.

### Seed 1. The Sniffari Passport (the daily walk; the dog's experience)

- **Real problem:** City walks are short, on pavement, and steered by the human, while smell is how dogs take in the world. In a small controlled study, two weeks of sniff-and-search games made dogs more "optimistic" on a standard test, and heel training did not (Duranton and Horowitz, 2019; moderate). Sniff gardens exist at shelters, including the ASPCA's in Manhattan (1.18), but this research found none open to owned dogs in New York City.
- **Fantasy version:** A collar lends the owner the dog's nose for ten minutes. The block turns into colored smell trails. The halal cart glows orange, yesterday's pug glows purple, and the dog picks the route.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Set up 8 "sniff stations" along one path in Riverside or Morningside Park: a pot of a dog-safe herb, a bag of autumn leaves, a towel rubbed on a cat, a bakery box. Owners get a paper passport and one rule: the dog leads and the human follows for 15 minutes. A volunteer stamps each station the dog chooses to investigate. Afterward, ask each owner what they noticed about their dog. Have a vet check every item for safety first.
- **Who it serves:** The dog, the owner, dog walkers.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, the walk stops being a chore to finish. Practical, a 15-minute enrichment ritual fits a workday. Identity, the owner shifts from "the person who walks the dog" to "the dog's guide in a world of smell". Social change is small.
- **Builds on:** Sensory gardens (1.18). Alexandra Horowitz's dog cognition lab at Barnard (see the evidence section) is a natural expert interview.

### Seed 2. The Pack Board and the Walk Pool (leaving the dog; trust)

- **Real problem:** Interview findings say owners need care during work, travel, and errands, and that they trust people who know the dog more than star ratings. In a four-city survey, about 40% of pet owners got some kind of social support from people they met through their pet (Wood et al., 2015; high).
- **Fantasy version:** A bone-shaped Bat-Signal. When an owner is stuck at work, they shine it over the building, and the neighbors the dog already loves rush out in capes. They call themselves the Leash League.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** In one apartment building, put a "Pack Board" in the lobby. Each dog gets a photo card, and under it go the names of neighbors the dog already knows and likes. Host a 30-minute "meet the pack" coffee in the lobby. Start a walk pool that works like a carpool (Monday I walk yours, Tuesday you walk mine), logged on the board with a pen.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, neighbors without dogs, dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, the owner gets backup care without a stranger. Emotional, the owner can stay late at work without panic. Social, the building becomes a village. Identity, a neighbor becomes "Biscuit's aunt".
- **Builds on:** PAWS NY's fixed weekly volunteers (1.23) and BorrowMyDoggy (1.24).

### Seed 3. The Dog Résumé (trust; handing the dog over; allergies)

- **Real problem:** Interview findings point to trust in people who know the dog and to confusion about food and allergies. When a dog changes hands (sitter, walker, neighbor), the critical facts live only in the owner's head.
- **Fantasy version:** The dog gives a TED talk about itself. "My name is Pickles. I fear umbrellas. Chicken makes me itch. Three humans can vouch for me."
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Write a one-page template in the dog's voice: daily routine, fears, food rules and allergies, what each tail position means, emergency contacts, and three "references" (people who know the dog, one sentence each). Test it with 5 owners and the sitters they actually use. Hand over the page and record every question the sitter still has to ask.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, sitters and walkers, dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, the handover is safe. Emotional, the owner has peace of mind while away. Social, references from real people replace star ratings. Identity, the dog is presented as an individual.

### Seed 4. Dog Coat Check (errands; drop-off spots)

- **Real problem:** The interviews asked for drop-off spots across the city. DogSpot had more than 40 dog boxes outside Brooklyn shops and more than 1,000 customer accounts before the city's Department of Transportation ordered them off public sidewalks in November 2017 (1.22; high). The demand was proven, and the public sidewalk was the blocker.
- **Fantasy version:** Every bodega has a dog valet in a red jacket. The owner hands over the leash and gets a ticket. On their return, the dog has had a shoulder rub and learned a little French.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** One Saturday morning, set a "coat check" table on the private frontage of a grocery store or cafe that agrees to host it. Use numbered tags, a water bowl, a 15-minute limit, and one volunteer for every two dogs. Count how many owners use it, how long they stay away, how many say they would pay, and how much.
- **Who it serves:** Owners running errands, and dogs that would otherwise wait tied to a pole.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, errands with the dog along. Emotional, no fear of theft or of a scared dog tied outside. Social, the valet learns the dogs' and owners' names.
- **Rule to respect:** Stay on private space with the host's permission. The 2017 order concerned objects on the public sidewalk.

### Seed 5. Speed-Sniffing (dog friendships and compatibility)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found that owners worry about which dogs their dog gets along with. In a survey of 13,715 dogs, 17% were highly fearful of other dogs (Salonen et al., 2020; high). Shelter trainers already sort dogs by play style in supervised playgroups (1.29).
- **Fantasy version:** A sorting hat for dogs. Two dogs touch noses, the hat glows, and it announces "best friends forever", "polite acquaintances", or "never again".
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Book a small fenced dog run at an off-peak hour, with a certified trainer present. Owners fill in a one-page "play style card" (chaser, wrestler, sniffer, ball addict, prefers people). The trainer pairs dogs by card for three-minute meetings through a fence, and lets them off leash only if both dogs stay relaxed. Each owner leaves with the names of 2 or 3 dogs that played well with theirs, plus those owners' phone numbers.
- **Who it serves:** Owners and dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, worry turns into a short list of known friends. Social, owners swap numbers because the dogs chose each other. Practical, playdates become safe to arrange. Identity, "my dog has friends".

### Seed 6. The Quiet Hour Club (reactive dogs)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found sensitive dogs who want no dog friends. In the Finnish survey, 29% of dogs were highly fearful (Salonen et al., 2020; high). Owners elsewhere pay to rent private yards for such dogs (Sniffspot, 1.26), but Manhattan has few yards. The ASPCA built a sniff meadow for fearful dogs at its Manhattan adoption center (1.18).
- **Fantasy version:** An invisibility cloak for the dog. Other dogs simply cannot see it, so it crosses Central Park in perfect calm. Or a force-field bubble pops up whenever another dog comes within 20 feet.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Find one enclosed space that sits empty at certain hours, such as a church courtyard, a school yard at 7 a.m., a restaurant backyard before opening, or a building roof. Offer 30-minute private slots through a paper sign-up sheet. Each member gets a yellow bandana (1.28) and a pocket card that says "We need space, thank you". After each slot, ask the owner to rate their own stress and the dog's.
- **Who it serves:** Reactive dogs and their owners.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, from shame and constant alertness to relief. Social, members meet at a humans-only coffee while their dogs never have to meet. Practical, the dog gets a safe place to run. Identity, from "owner of the bad dog" to "member of a club".

### Seed 7. The Bark Car (getting around the city)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found big dogs shut out. The subway admits only animals in a container, so New Yorkers carry big dogs in IKEA bags (1.21). Japan's rail companies have run special trains where dogs ride out of carriers (1.10).
- **Fantasy version:** On weekends, the last car of every train becomes the Bark Car, with a fire-hydrant pole, a water fountain, and a Bernese mountain dog as conductor.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Two options. First, a "Big Dog Bag Couture" show at a dog run, where owners model their subway-legal carriers, a trainer shows safe lifting, and the crowd votes. Second, a cardboard Bark Car in a park with a short survey ("Which trips can you not make with your dog today?"). The survey answers are the real output.
- **Who it serves:** Big-dog owners and their dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, safer carrying and a clear list of blocked trips. Identity, big-dog owners stop feeling like an inconvenience and become a visible group with a demand. Social, they find each other.
- **Caution:** Carrying a 70-pound dog down subway stairs can injure people and dogs. The event must not encourage unsafe carrying.

### Seed 8. Big Dogs Welcome Here (big dogs and "dog-friendly" places)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found big dogs shut out of "dog-friendly" places. Size is a common hidden filter in New York; public housing caps dogs at 25 pounds (evidence table).
- **Fantasy version:** A shrink ray at every shop door turns a Great Dane into a pocket dog until checkout. Or an embassy where big dogs enjoy diplomatic immunity.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Walk one shopping strip (for example, Broadway from 110th to 125th Street) with a big, calm dog and ask 30 businesses, "Can this dog come in?" Record yes, no, and the reason. Print a one-page map and a "Big Dogs Welcome" window sticker for each yes. A week later, ask big-dog owners whether they went.
- **Who it serves:** Big-dog owners and small businesses.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, a list of places to go. Identity, from apologizing for the dog's size to being welcomed. Social, shop staff learn the dog's name.
- **Note:** Food businesses must follow health rules on animals, so expect many refusals, and record the reasons as data.

### Seed 9. The Paw Forecast (weather)

- **Real problem:** The interviews named bad weather. In the sun, asphalt can be 40 to 60°F hotter than the air (around 125°F on a 77°F day), and paw pads can burn at about 120 to 125°F. In winter, sidewalk salt cracks and burns paws (evidence table; moderate).
- **Fantasy version:** Every dog gets a personal cloud that rains on hot pavement and melts ice on cold days. Or every sidewalk gets a heated "paw lane".
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Put a chalkboard at a dog run gate or a bodega and update it morning and evening. It shows the asphalt temperature from a cheap infrared thermometer, a green, yellow, or red paw, and one tip ("walk the grass side of Riverside Drive today"). On the worst days, run a pop-up "indoor sniff hour" in a building lobby or community room.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, walkers, dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, the right time and route for the walk. Emotional, less guilt about a short walk. Social, the board becomes a meeting point.
- **Builds on:** Boris & Horton (1.27) shows the pull of an indoor dog space and how hard it is to sustain one.

### Seed 10. The "Is This Normal?" Desk (health and food confusion)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found confusion about food, allergies, and what is normal. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration investigated a possible link between grain-free, legume-heavy dog foods and a heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy) from July 2018, received 1,382 reports, and stopped regular updates in December 2022 without proving or ruling out a cause (evidence table; high). Owners got years of alarm and no verdict.
- **Fantasy version:** A poop sommelier in a velvet jacket sniffs, swirls, and announces, "A confident stool, notes of chicken, no cause for concern." Or a talking bowl reads every food label aloud and translates it.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Put a folding table at a dog run, staffed by a volunteer veterinary technician or veterinary student, with three posters: a stool chart, a body-shape chart, and "how to read a food label". Owners bring their food bag and their worry. The desk sorts every question into one of three answers: "normal", "ask your vet this week", or "go now". Write down every question asked, because the list itself is research data.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, especially first-time owners, and their dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, less anxiety. Practical, a clear next step. Identity, from anxious owner to competent owner.
- **Caution:** Only a licensed veterinarian can diagnose or treat. The desk sorts and refers, and it never prescribes.

### Seed 11. The Paw Fund (cost)

- **Real problem:** The interviews named rising vet and insurance costs. Prices for pet services including vet care rose 5.1% in the year to February 2026, against 2.4% for all prices, and vet visits fell for 16 straight quarters (Fortune, using Bureau of Labor Statistics data; high). 31% of pet owners aged 50 to 80 say their pet strains their budget, up from 18% in 2018 (University of Michigan poll; high).
- **Fantasy version:** Every dog has a video-game health bar floating above its head, and neighbors toss coins at it to refill it after a vet bill.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Ten owners in one building each put $10 a month into a shared, transparent jar or account for surprise vet bills, with one page of rules and a paper ledger on the lobby board. Pair it with a "group wellness day": ask a nearby clinic whether it will give a group rate for several dogs booked together.
- **Who it serves:** Owners on tight budgets and older owners.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, a cushion for a bad day. Emotional, less dread of the vet. Social, neighbors help each other. Identity, from facing costs alone to being part of a pack.
- **Caution:** A fund that promises payouts may count as insurance under state law. Keep it a voluntary gift circle with no guarantees, and get legal advice before it grows.

### Seed 12. The Paw-ssport Office (travel)

- **Real problem:** The interviews named international travel paperwork. Since 2024-08-01, every dog entering the U.S. must be at least 6 months old, carry an ISO microchip implanted before any required rabies shot, and arrive with a CDC Dog Import Form receipt (CDC; high). The EU requires the rabies shot at 12 weeks or older plus a 21-day wait, and it adopted a new passport model in 2026 (1.25; high). One step done in the wrong order can void a vaccine.
- **Fantasy version:** An embassy for dogs at JFK airport. The dog presents its paw print, the consul stamps a bone-shaped visa, and an honor guard of beagles escorts it to the gate.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Hold a "Passport Photo Day" at a dog run. A photographer takes a passport-style portrait, a volunteer with a handheld scanner confirms that the microchip reads, and each owner leaves with a paper booklet. The booklet is a personal timeline that counts back from the travel date (chip, then rabies shot, then the 21-day wait, then forms), with a box to stamp as each step is done.
- **Who it serves:** Owners who travel or move abroad, international students with pets, dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, no missed step. Emotional, confidence before a stressful trip. Identity, the dog becomes "a citizen of the world" with a keepsake to prove it.
- **Builds on:** The EU pet passport (1.25) and BARK Air (1.1).

### Seed 13. Borrowed Legs (elderly owners)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found elderly owners who cannot walk their dogs. In the University of Michigan poll, 6% of older pet owners said a pet had caused them a fall or injury (2018 data), and 83% said their pet gives them a sense of purpose (2025 data). PAWS NY has helped more than 1,500 older adults and people with illness or disability keep their pets since 2009 (1.23).
- **Fantasy version:** The owner gets bionic legs that work only during walk time. Or the leash connects to a gentle drone that the owner steers from the window, like flying a kite.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Through a senior center or a church in Morningside Heights, pair 3 student volunteers with 3 older owners. The volunteer walks the dog and brings back a "walk postcard" with three phone photos, the route, the dog's best sniff, and who they met. The owner joins for the first block or watches from the window. Approach PAWS NY as a partner, not a competitor.
- **Who it serves:** Older owners, their dogs, and students who cannot own a dog.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, the owner keeps the dog and the sense of purpose. Social, a friendship across generations. Identity, the owner stays "Biscuit's person" instead of becoming someone who had to give a dog up. Practical, the dog gets real exercise.

### Seed 14. The Dog Library (people who cannot own dogs)

- **Real problem:** Many New Yorkers cannot own a dog because of leases, the 25-pound public housing limit, long hours, or travel. Demand for time with other people's dogs runs high in cities. BorrowMyDoggy reports over 1 million members in the UK and Ireland and up to 100 borrowers per owner in some city areas (1.24; moderate). Hotel guests booked walks with a hotel dog three months ahead (1.11).
- **Fantasy version:** The public library lends dogs like books. Borrowers get a card and a due date, and the dog comes back with notes in the margin ("prefers the hydrant on 112th Street").
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Run a pop-up "Dog Library" desk at a dog run on a Sunday. Five owners each lend their dog for a 20-minute walk to a borrower they have just met at the desk. Use real library cards, a date stamp, and a return slip with one sentence about the walk.
- **Who it serves:** People without dogs, owners (who get free help), dogs.
- **How the person changes:** Identity, from "I can't have a dog" to "I am a dog person". Emotional, joy. Social, owners and borrowers become neighbors. Practical, owners build a pool of known helpers, which feeds Seed 2.

### Seed 15. The Shelter Dog Day-Out Diary (shelter dogs)

- **Real problem:** Kennel life stresses shelter dogs. ACC's BoroughBreaks let New Yorkers take a large shelter dog out for up to a day. In the 2019 launch months, about 100 dogs went out and about a dozen were adopted as a direct result (1.13; high). VCA Charities claims that dogs who spend a day out are 10 times more likely to be adopted (VCA's own claim; moderate). Slots fill fast, and good stories about each dog are scarce.
- **Fantasy version:** Each shelter dog gets one perfect New York day: a ride on the Staten Island Ferry, a bacon, egg, and cheese, and a standing ovation in Times Square.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Book real BoroughBreak slots for team members. Each takes one dog out alone and follows ACC's rules (leash on, no dog parks, no other dogs). Each fills in a "Day-Out Diary" postcard with three photos and five facts ("loves couches, ignores pigeons, rides elevators like a pro"). Share the diaries with ACC for adoption profiles and count the inquiries.
- **Who it serves:** Shelter dogs, people who cannot own a dog, ACC.
- **How the person changes:** Identity, from "I can't adopt" to "I helped a dog get home". Emotional, joy and purpose. Practical, adopters get real information about the dog.

### Seed 16. The Last Walk (pet loss)

- **Real problem:** In a PLOS ONE study of 975 British adults, 21% of people who had lost both a pet and a person found the pet's death harder, and about 7.5% of people who lost a pet met the criteria for prolonged grief disorder (Hyland et al., reported 2026-01-15; high). Others often fail to recognize pet grief, and this research found no U.S. state that requires leave when a pet dies.
- **Fantasy version:** Once a year, the dog's old route glows at night, and a dog made of light trots along it, stopping at every favorite tree. Or every New York dog that dies gets a star in a constellation above Central Park.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Make a "Last Walk" kit. The owner invites the people who knew the dog to walk its favorite route once more. At each favorite spot they draw a chalk paw print. At the end they write the dog's name on a tag and tie it to a "memory board" at a dog run (with the run's permission), or they write in a shared book. Offer a monthly pet loss circle at a cafe for those who want more.
- **Who it serves:** Grieving owners, and the walkers and neighbors who knew the dog and grieve too.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, the grief gets witnesses. Social, the neighborhood acknowledges the loss. Identity, from "the person whose dog died" to "the keeper of the dog's story".
- **Builds on:** Hartsdale Pet Cemetery (1.14), robot dog funerals (1.15), and Laurie Anderson's "Heart of a Dog" (1.3).

### Seed 17. The Carrier Makeover Party (cats)

- **Real problem:** In 2024, 74.2% of dog owners but only 57.3% of cat owners visited a veterinary practice (AVMA figures; moderate). Fights with the carrier and the car ride are a commonly cited reason for skipping cat visits (moderate).
- **Fantasy version:** A cat teleporter. The cat steps into a cardboard box at home, as it would anyway, and reappears, unbothered, on the vet's table.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Hold a Saturday workshop at a library or pet store. Cat owners bring their carriers, decorate them, and learn carrier training: the carrier stays open at home as a bed, with treats and a familiar blanket inside. Each owner takes home a 14-day sticker calendar. Two weeks later, ask whether the cat now walks in on its own.
- **Who it serves:** Cat owners and cats.
- **How the person changes:** Practical, vet visits become possible. Emotional, less dread and guilt. Social and identity, cat owners get an event of their own, since most pet events are built for dogs.
- **Builds on:** Meow Parlour (1.30) and the NY Cat Film Festival (1.4), which show that cat people turn up for cat events.

### Seed 18. Dog-Cam Cinema (the dog's own experience of the city)

- **Real problem:** Owners cannot see the walk from 18 inches up. In the Finnish survey, 32% of dogs were highly sensitive to noise, 15% feared strangers, and 11% feared new situations (Salonen et al., 2020; high). Hot asphalt and sidewalk salt are hard to notice from human height (evidence table).
- **Fantasy version:** The owner turns into their own dog for one hour, Ben 10 style, and walks the block at knee height, hearing every siren at full volume and smelling every corner.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Fix a phone or small action camera at dog height (on a harness, or held low on a stick) for one walk per owner. Screen the clips at an outdoor "Dog-Cam Cinema" night in a park, in the style of the NY Dog Film Festival. Ask each owner, "What surprised you?" Collect a block-by-block list of hazards (scaffolding, broken glass, salt, sirens).
- **Who it serves:** Owners, dogs, and any group that pushes the city to fix sidewalks.
- **How the person changes:** Emotional, empathy for the dog. Identity, from walker to the dog's advocate. Social, a shared screening.
- **Builds on:** The NY Dog Film Festival (1.4) and Kenya Hara's "scale modifier" idea (1.6).

### Seed 19. Dog Mayor of the Block (neighbors; names)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found that owners know dogs' names before owners' names. Pet owners had higher odds of getting to know neighbors (odds ratio 1.61), and about 40% got social support from people they met through their pet (Wood et al., 2015; high).
- **Fantasy version:** Every block elects a dog mayor with real powers. It can declare a belly-rub holiday, veto jackhammers during nap time, and cut the ribbon on new fire hydrants.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** One block, one week. Campaign posters at a bodega or a dog run show the dog's photo, its platform ("more squirrels, fewer baths"), and the owner's first name in big letters. Votes cost $1 in a cash jar, and the money goes to ACC. A Saturday inauguration ends the week with a sash and a speech.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, neighbors without dogs, shelter dogs (through the money raised).
- **How the person changes:** Social, the ballot puts owners' names next to their dogs' names. Identity, block pride, and people without dogs become voters and campaign staff. Emotional, shared silliness.
- **Builds on:** Rabbit Hash, Kentucky (1.5), where the 2024 election drew 14,162 votes for the winner and raised over $26,000.

### Seed 20. Silent Disco for Dogs (social life without another app)

- **Real problem:** The interviews found fatigue with yet another app, and owners who know dogs' names but not owners' names. Loud events exclude many dogs, since 32% are highly sensitive to noise (Salonen et al., 2020; high).
- **Fantasy version:** A rooftop concert where the band is all dogs, the dogs sit in the front row, and the humans wear headphones at the back.
- **Stage it tomorrow:** Hold a one-hour session in a park at dusk. Humans wear wireless headphones, dogs hear a low-volume speaker, and a student violinist or a playlist provides the music. Name tags show the owner's name in big letters and the dog's name in small letters, which reverses how people usually meet at the dog run. End with a group howl, as Laurie Anderson ended her Times Square concert with the dogs barking together (1.3). Use a paper sign-up sheet and no app.
- **Who it serves:** Owners, dogs, and people without dogs who come to watch.
- **How the person changes:** Social, owners learn each other's names. Emotional, shared joy. Identity, "we are this park's dog people".
- **Builds on:** Laurie Anderson's Concert for Dogs (1.3).

Seven seeds tied at 14. Three tie-breakers decided the top five. First, the problem must show up in the team's own interviews and be felt weekly, not once a year. ### 1. Speed-Sniffing (Seed 5): score 15

- **Why it wins:** It answers the first problem listed in the team's interview findings, and outside data back it (17% of dogs highly fearful of other dogs). Dogs choosing their friends is delightful to watch. The single service fits in one sentence, "we find your dog's friends". One dog run and about 8 dogs create value on day one. The change is visible in every dimension, from worry to confidence and from strangers to owners who swap numbers.
- **Strongest objection:** Dog runs are free, and owners mix dogs there every day without help. A session also carries real risk of a fight, so it needs a paid trainer and a liability plan.
- **Answer to the objection:** Unstructured mixing at the free dog run is the likely source of the worry the interviews describe (an inference, to check against the interview notes). The structure (play-style card, fence first, trainer present) is what the free run lacks.
- **One-day test and success signal:** 8 dogs in one session. Success means at least 3 owner pairs swap numbers, and at least 2 playdates happen within a week without the team arranging them.

### 2. The Quiet Hour Club (Seed 6): score 14

- **Why it wins:** It serves the other half of the same interview finding, the sensitive dogs who want no friends. Evidence is strong (29% of dogs highly fearful), and owners elsewhere already pay for private space (Sniffspot). One borrowed courtyard is enough to start. The identity change is the deepest in the list, from "owner of the bad dog" to "member of a club".
- **Strongest objection:** The market is a niche, and empty enclosed space in Manhattan is rare and comes with insurance and liability questions. Sniffspot could also simply expand into New York.
- **Answer to the objection:** The interview finding about sensitive dogs points to an intense, recurring need, and a niche with intense need is a good place to prove a first service. Sniffspot's model depends on private yards, which Manhattan lacks.
- **One-day test and success signal:** Post 6 slots in one space with a paper sheet and a flyer at two vet offices. Success means the slots fill within 48 hours and owners rate their own stress lower after the slot than before it.

### 3. The Pack Board and the Walk Pool (Seed 2): score 14

- **Why it wins:** It turns two interview findings (care during work and travel, and trust in people who know the dog) into one service that runs every week. The network is one building, which already exists. Research shows pets already connect neighbors (odds ratio 1.61 for getting to know neighbors).
- **Strongest objection:** Neighbors already trade favors informally, and if a dog gets hurt on a neighbor's watch, the friendship and the board both collapse. Building managers may also refuse a lobby board.
- **Answer to the objection:** Informal swaps happen only between people who already know each other. The board and the coffee create the first contact that the swaps need.
- **One-day test and success signal:** One building. Success means at least 5 dogs go on the board and at least 2 walk swaps get logged within a week.

### 4. The "Is This Normal?" Desk (Seed 10): score 14

- **Why it wins:** Food and health confusion came up in the interviews, and the national grain-free scare left owners without a verdict for years. The poop sommelier is funny enough to draw a crowd. One table at one dog run delivers value in the first hour. The change is an owner who feels competent instead of anxious.
- **Strongest objection:** Giving health advice without a veterinary license is a legal and ethical risk, vets may see the desk as competition, and online forums already answer these questions for free.
- **Answer to the objection:** The desk sorts and refers, so a clinic can treat it as a source of new clients. Conflicting answers online are one likely source of the confusion the interviews describe.
- **One-day test and success signal:** One table for two hours with a volunteer veterinary technician. Success means 15 or more questions and a higher self-rated confidence score after the visit than before it.

### 5. Dog Mayor of the Block (Seed 19): score 14

- **Why it wins:** It has the highest delight per dollar in the list, and a real precedent proves the mechanics (Rabbit Hash raised over $26,000 with $1 votes). It attacks the "dogs' names before owners' names" finding head-on by printing both on the ballot. It is the best "before a network" engine, because the election itself recruits the block that the other seeds need.
- **Strongest objection:** It is a fun event, not a service, and it does not fix a painful problem. The delight may fade after one week.
- **Answer to the objection:** That is why it ranks fifth. Its job is to launch the primary service, not to be it.
- **One-day test and success signal:** One block for one week. Success means at least 10 candidate dogs, at least 100 votes, and owners who can name three other owners afterward.

### Recommendation for the midterm (moderate confidence; a judgment, not a finding)

- **Prove one service first, "the right company for your dog".** Run Speed-Sniffing for social dogs. Treat the Quiet Hour Club as the same service's answer for dogs whose right company is nobody. Both answer the most specific interview finding in the brief (compatibility worries and sensitive dogs), and both run at one site with no app.
- **Use Dog Mayor of the Block as the launch event**, not as the product. It recruits the first block and gets owners' names onto paper.
- **Keep the Pack Board as the next chapter.** Dogs that become friends lead to owners who trust each other, and that trust is what the walk pool needs.
- **The strongest case against this recommendation:** The Pack Board may answer a bigger daily pain (care during work hours) and earn more often than an occasional matching session. If the team's interview notes show care during work as the most frequent complaint, the Pack Board should lead instead.
- **What not to do:** Do not pitch all five as features of one app. The professor warned against "an app that does it all", and owners in the interviews reported fatigue with yet another app.

## Method and limits

- **Searches:** 46 web searches and page fetches, run on 2026-10-20.
- **How facts were checked:** Most facts came from search-tool summaries of the named pages. Pages fetched and read directly: Fortune on vet prices, the European Commission pet travel page, The Conversation on the pet grief study, and Wikipedia on Alexandra Horowitz. Before putting any number on a slide, a team member should open the cited link and confirm it.
- **Not verified in this research:** Best Friends Animal Society's "Adventure Buddy" program in SoHo; whether DogSpot operates in New York City in 2026 and what came of the City Council "pet harbor" pilot; Boris & Horton's 2026 status (based on listings, not news); the 2026 NY Dog Film Festival premiere date and venue; the BARK Air 2026 schedule (seen through search results for the booking page); whether New Yorkers recognize the yellow ribbon; the AVMA cat and dog visit figures (seen through a secondary summary); the dollar value of a Sniffspot "credit"; and whether any public sniff garden for owned dogs exists in New York City.
- **Not covered:** Dog cafes in Japan (Boris & Horton stands in as the New York example), consumer "bark translator" apps, dog mayors in towns other than Rabbit Hash, and Kimpton or other hotel pet-lending programs beyond Fairmont.
- **Searched for and not found:** Any government ceremony that grants a dog "citizenship". The EU pet passport and the New York City dog license are the closest real bases.
- **Sources:** Every source is cited inline next to the claim it supports, with its URL and date where the page gives one.
