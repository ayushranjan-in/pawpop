# Course: User Centric Design and Innovation (Columbia). Assignments and readings

Transcribed on 2026-10-20 from the owner's Canvas screenshots in `canvas/`. The screenshots are the source of truth; check them if a line here looks wrong. Some prompts are cut off at the screenshot edge, marked "[cut off]".

## Assignments, in order

| Week | Due | Points | Prompt (condensed, key phrases kept) | AI allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Sep 21, 11:59pm | 5 | Form a team of 3 to 5. In Miro, develop a research plan: who you need to talk to and how many customers, users, or stakeholders; how you will interview and listen; what you heard; what you want to redesign. Also a team charter (behaviors, responsibilities, expectations, conflict). Graded on the quality of insights from interviews: the interviewee's context, stated needs and desires, the 5 kinds of value as they apply, and "your insights (from your perspective) of what you observe (not merely what they state)". | not stated |
| 3 | Sep 28, 11:59pm | 5 | In Miro, interview at least 8 users and report findings. Who have you not heard from? What more do you need to explore? Ideally interview others in the stakeholder ecosystem to find the value flowing around the ecosystem map; "doing this from only your perspective severely limits the scope and veracity of what goes into the map." 4 to 5 hours. | No AI |
| 4 | Oct 5, 11:59pm | 5 | Expand research by 2 more customers each. Research at least 2 key stakeholders. Draft a stakeholder value map showing how the 5 kinds of value flow among stakeholders, plus trust, communication, and data. Best opportunities and threats? What to explore? Partnership strategy? Graded on: the strategic plan, integration of stakeholders from the map, identified trends, competitors and "ratings of decision-drivers"; results should be key opportunities, threats, and an overall favorable position, which should align. Capabilities, priorities, and partners optional. | No AI |
| 5 | Oct 12, 11:59pm | 5 | In Miro, identify opportunities and threats and how they reframe the project. Major insights, opportunities, suggested responses. Prepare a presentation on where you've been, what you've learned, how your direction changed (link it on the Miro board). Also draft a waveline of the existing customer experience, as evidenced by research, and chart an ideal one, "the best possible customer experience you can imagine". The grading text on this screenshot speaks of the user-interface map, wireframes, and screen renders: "how users move within it, navigation, and potential actions on each screen". | No AI |
| 6 | Oct 19, 11:59pm | 10 | "Use AI design/solution tools to create a preliminary solution and post it to Miro. Document which tools you [used]." Simultaneously, "create a draft customer waveline (value map) for next week." Graded on understanding of the material and its purpose (its relation to innovation), and on your interpretation of the material for your projects, work, and career: which tools will integrate easily with your skills and which will challenge you. | Yes: "use AI to create your prototype as needed; notate the tool(s) you used" |
| 7 | Tuesday by 6pm (owner says: the day after 2026-10-19, so 2026-10-20) | 5 | Midterm presentation, submitted as a website URL or a file. "Create a summary presentation of what you've learned to date and how it has impacted your semester project (and your own worklife). This should be at least 10 slides and you will have 7 minutes to present." 1 to 2 hours. Graded on course concepts to date, the tools, and their use, but "I'm looking for your interpretation of their use and impact and not merely a summary... How do you assess their importance and viability and how do you envision using them, currently, or in the future?" | not stated |

Every weekly prompt repeats one grading theme: "this class is about your personal development in becoming an innovator, whether you perform design activities in the future or work with others who do."

## Readings and videos, by week

| Week | Required | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Auernhammer and Roth (2021), origin and evolution of Stanford's design thinking, JPIM 38(6) 623-644. Shedroff (2021) videos: Design thinking; Great design; Typography. Shedroff (2025) video: Experience dimensions. Staats, How to give powerful (and effective) design critique. | Berkun, How design makes the world; Brown, Change by design; Liedtka and Ogilvie, Designing for growth |
| 2 | Guest, Bunce, Johnson (2006), How many interviews are enough? Field Methods 18(1). Ireland (2003), Qualitative methods: from giving voice to interpreting meaning. Shedroff (2021) videos: 5 kinds of value; Stakeholder analysis. Shedroff (2023), A whole new strategy, ch. 1 "Your customers". | Hall, Just enough research; Portigal, Interviewing users |
| 3 | Beaujean, Davidson, Madge (2006), The "moment of truth" in customer service, McKinsey Quarterly. Jones and Samalionis (2008), From small ideas to radical service innovation, Design Management Review 19(1). Fogg (2009), Fogg behavior model (behaviormodel.org) and A behavior model for persuasive design (Persuasive 2009). Shedroff (2023), ch. 10 "The world around you". Review artifact: stakeholder value maps. | Meadows, Thinking in systems |
| 4 | Shedroff (2021) video: Fixing the SWOT analysis. Shedroff (2023), A whole new strategy, introduction and ch. 2 to 4. Shedroff (2025) video: Continuous strategy. Review artifact: design-based strategic plans. | Van Der Pijl et al., Design a better business |
| 5 | Brenchley (2020), Interaction metaphors. IxDF (2023), Modes and modal interfaces. Process Masterclass, A no-bullshit primer on UX design. Shedroff (2025) video: The waveline. They Make Design (2023), What is affordance in UX? Wodtke (2018), Foundations of interaction design. | none listed |
| 6 | Mehta (2023), A primer on usability testing. Moran (2019), Usability testing 101 (NN/g). Re-read Staats on design critique. | none listed |
